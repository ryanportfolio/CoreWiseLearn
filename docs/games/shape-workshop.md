# Shape Workshop

A creative game in the Rainbow Workshop world. The child fits paper shapes into the faint outline of a picture; the finished picture comes alive and goes onto the child's own gallery shelf. A free-build sheet lets the child stamp shapes anywhere in any colour. Game id `shape-workshop`, `mode: 'creative'`, `learning: ['shapes']`.

## The action and its response

- **Build a picture.** The workbench shows a sheet of cream paper with a picture (rocket, fish, house, cat, car, flower). Some parts of the picture are already glued down in colour; the open parts are faint dashed outlines. A small part the game glues on itself that sits on an open part (the fish's eye and stripe on its body, the rocket's heart on its body, the house's door and windows on its wall) waits off the sheet and pops on together with the part under it. A part the child (or the helper hand) places never waits: it stays on the sheet from the moment it lands, in whatever order the parts go in, and a part landing underneath it later slides in below it. Loose paper shapes lie in a tray beside the sheet. The child drags a shape onto the matching outline, or clicks the shape and then clicks the outline.
- **Match.** The piece snaps in: it stretches and turns to fit the outline, lands with a small slam and dip, a ring of paper sparkles bursts out, and a woody pop plays with a paper rustle. Each landed piece in a picture plays one note higher on the pentatonic scale. The pieces left in the tray slide up to close the gap.
- **Where outlines overlap.** Some outlines sit on or inside others (the rocket's window on its body, the cat's ears on its head, the car's wheels on its body). A piece dropped inside an outline of its own shape always goes in, whatever other outlines lie under it; when several outlines of its shape hold the drop point, the innermost (smallest) one takes it.
- **Not a match.** Dropped only on an outline of another shape, the piece gives a quick side-to-side wiggle and floats back to its place in the tray with a soft, low sound. Dropped on empty paper, it simply floats back. Nothing scolds and nothing is lost.
- **Picture done.** A short pause, a wave of little jumps across the parts, a fanfare and paper confetti, then the picture comes alive for about three seconds: the rocket rumbles and launches off the top and lands back; the fish swims with a flicking tail and rising bubbles; the house opens its door, lights its windows and puffs smoke; the cat stretches long, yawns and swishes its tail; the car spins its wheels, drives off the right edge and comes back from the left; the flower spins its star petals, waves its leaves and smiles. Then the picture shrinks and flies into the gallery button, which bounces. The next picture's outline and pieces deal in. The save counts the finished picture and makes the next picture current the moment the last piece lands, so leaving at any point during the celebration comes back to the next picture.
- **Free build.** A clean paper sheet with a tray of seven shapes and seven colour swatches. Clicking the sheet stamps the chosen shape in the chosen colour with a slam, a slight random turn and size, and a pop pitched by shape. Holding the button down and moving stamps a trail. The trail ends when the button comes up, and also when the window loses focus or the press is cancelled without the release reaching the page, so moving the pointer with no button held never stamps. A piece being dragged in build mode floats back to the tray in the same case. Clicking a swatch recolours the tray. On a portrait screen too narrow for seven swatches, one swatch cell steps to the next colour on each click.
- **Fresh paper.** A round lilac button in free build shows a blank paper sheet with a small yellow brush. Pressing it empties the sheet: a brush as tall as the sheet sweeps across it from left to right in 0.65 s with a whoosh, the stamps vanish behind its bristles, paper bits fly up off its front, and a few sparkles land on the clean sheet. Other sheets and the finished pictures are untouched. Presses on the button, the sheet or the keys during the sweep do nothing; stamping works again the moment it ends. On a sheet that is already empty the button only wiggles with a soft tok.
- **Gallery.** Two paper shelves. The top shelf holds the six pictures: finished ones in colour, gently moving (the fish swims in its frame, the rocket hovers), unfinished ones as faint outlines. The bottom shelf holds six free-build sheets as small previews. Clicking any frame opens it: a picture to build (again), or a sheet to keep stamping.

## Discovery without words

- First visit: a paper helper hand slides in, picks up the first open piece, carries it to its outline and places it. That placement is real and counts as part of the picture. The hand slides away.
- First visit to free build: the hand taps a shape, then taps the sheet twice; two real stamps appear.
- Idle in build (9 s without a placement, then every 12 s): the hand taps a piece that has an open outline, then glides to that outline and taps it; both pulse. When the child has already picked up a piece by clicking it, the hand glides straight to that piece's outline and taps it. The child still makes the move.
- A piece held for 1 s (picked up by a click and not yet placed, or dragged) lights its nearest matching outline with a pulsing ring.
- Finished pictures fly into the gallery button, so the child sees where the pictures go.

## Creative, not rounds

No stars, no score, no rounds, no forced finish. A half-built picture and every free-build sheet are saved when the child leaves (Home, the break nudge, closing the tab after the save), and come back exactly as they were. The finished pictures and the sheets are the collection; there are no stickers. Completing a picture is the natural break point, so `services.roundBoundary()` is called when the picture has landed in the gallery button, letting a due break nudge appear between pictures, never during one. Creative play gets the same break points at its own pauses: leaving a free-build sheet (for the gallery or a picture), coming back to the gallery, and three quiet seconds after the last stamp or fresh-paper clear on a free-build sheet. A stamp or a clear during that wait cancels it, and the next break point needs another three quiet seconds. The call waits 0.35 s after the pause and until no button or piece is held, so a nudge never lands mid-gesture; if a new picture has already started by then, that picture's finish is the next break point instead. When the workshop is back on top after the nudge, its input guard starts over exactly as on entry (0.32 s in which keys and clicks on the workbench do nothing) and no top button stays ringed, so a run of keys that dismisses the nudge does nothing here for its first 0.32 s and never presses a button.

## Motor tiers (hidden, between pictures only)

| Tier | Open pieces per picture | Picture scale | Snap distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 3 | 100 percent of the sheet | 1.5 times the spot radius, at least 90 px |
| 1 | 5 | 95 percent | 1.25 times, at least 75 px |
| 2 | up to 8 | 90 percent | 1.1 times, at least 64 px |

Every outline keeps a hit area of at least 96 CSS px on its shortest side, whatever its drawn size. A drop counts as inside a matching outline when it lands within the drawn outline plus a 12 px rim, or within 48 px of the outline's centre; that check comes first. A drop inside only an outline of another shape is the gentle miss. A drop on bare paper still snaps into the nearest matching outline within the snap distance; the game chose that outline, so it records no learning evidence. A placement attempt with the pointer counts for motor skill: landing inside or within the snap distance of a matching outline is a hit; letting go between one and 2.5 snap distances from the nearest matching outline is a motor miss; letting go far away is not an attempt. After each picture: three or more attempts with more than a third missed drops a tier; a picture with no motor misses and every piece placed by the pointer counts toward a rise, and two such pictures in a row raise the tier.

## Learning progression (separate from motor)

Shapes: circle, square, triangle, rectangle, star, heart, semicircle. The open parts of a picture are chosen with as many different shapes as possible, biggest first, rotating the choice each time a picture is rebuilt.

| Level | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | The tray holds exactly the open pieces. Only upright parts are open, and a rectangle piece lies the way its outline stands (tall for the rocket body and the house door), so no turn is needed. |
| 1 | Two extra shapes that fit nowhere join the tray. Rectangles still lie the way their outlines stand. |
| 2 | Turned parts (the fish tail, the rocket fins, the upside-down star sun, the cat's upside-down heart nose) can be open: the upright piece in the tray must be recognised in its turned outline, and it turns as it snaps in. |

Learning evidence comes only from pointer placements the child makes unaided: a piece dropped inside the drawn matching outline it goes into, without first trying a wrong outline, is a hit (a drop that places only through the 12 px rim or the 48 px centre reach records nothing, because where outlines nest it may have been aimed at the outline around it); a drop or click on an outline of a different shape is a miss. Keyboard placements (which choose the matching outline for the child), drops on bare paper that snap into an outline, the demonstration, and placements within 8 s of a hint for that shape record nothing. A held piece's outline lighting up counts as a hint for its shape, so a piece placed within a second of picking it up still counts and one placed while its outline is lit does not, however long it was held. The shape's hint time is set when the light comes on and again when it goes off (the piece is let go, put back or placed), so a piece of that shape placed within 8 s after the light went off records nothing either. Each shape keeps its own hint time, so a later hint for another shape never clears it. The keyboard ring on an outline is a hint too: a shape's hint time is set when the ring arrives on its outline and again when the ring leaves it (it moves on, or the mouse takes over), so a piece placed by mouse while its outline is ringed or within 8 s after records nothing. After each picture: three or more hits and no misses counts toward a level rise, and two such pictures raise the level; three or more misses outnumbering the hits lowers it. Per-shape hit and miss counts are kept in the save for later voice and progress work.

## Keyboard-only play

- Build: the first open piece in the tray has a thick dark-and-light ring from the start, and its matching outline pulses with a bright ring and a small arrow. Arrow keys move the ring around the tray grid. Any other key (letters, Space, Enter, Tab, Escape) flies the ringed piece into its outline, and the ring moves on to the next piece that fits an outline, skipping the extra shapes. Only the arrow keys can ring an extra shape; any other key then makes it hop toward the sheet and back with a soft sound, and the ring moves on to a piece that fits. Mashing one key always finishes the picture, and no key leaves the picture or rings a top button while it is being built.
- Picture done: once the alive animation can be moved on (1.2 s in), Tab reaches the top buttons as below; any other key moves the animation on. A new picture clears the ring.
- Free build: a ring cursor sits in the middle of the sheet, shown only after a key is pressed (moving the mouse hides it again), so a mouse player never sees a ring that looks like a target. Holding an arrow key moves it. Any other key, Escape included, stamps at the cursor, moves the cursor one step along a wandering path (the heading turns a little at random, is pulled gently toward the emptiest part of the sheet, and bounces off the sheet's edges) and moves the tray choice to the next shape (and every so often the next colour), so key mashing spreads a varied collage across the sheet.
- Fresh paper (free build): Tab reaches the fresh paper button after the speaker. Once its ring has been in sight for 0.4 s, any key except Tab and the arrows presses it, and the ring drops so the next key stamps again. A key sooner than that drops the ring and stamps, so a fast run of Tabs and keys never clears the sheet.
- Gallery: the picture on the workbench is ringed; arrows move between frames; any other key opens the ringed frame. Escape goes Home.
- Top buttons (free build, gallery, and a finished picture as above): Tab moves the ring to Home, the mode buttons and the speaker in turn and back to play; arrows move it between them. Only Enter presses a ringed button, and only once the ring has been in sight for 0.4 s; Space, an earlier Enter or any other key drops the ring and plays. So Tab then Enter in a fast run of keys never presses Home. Any mouse button works as a click.

## Screen layout

Top row: Home at the top left; at the top right the speaker. Both take Bubble Bay's corner place and size at every screen size (radius 60 px at 1366x768 and 48 px at 800x600 and 390x600, 12 px from the edges), so the break nudge's own speaker covers this one exactly; the mode buttons share that size. Beside the speaker sit the gallery button (paper shelf with frames) and, in build mode, the free-build button (paper brush with shapes). On screens too narrow for four buttons in a row, the two mode buttons leave the top row in build mode: on a portrait screen they stand in a column under the speaker, beside the sheet, when that leaves a bigger sheet than a second row above it would (and the column ends above the tray); otherwise they drop to a second row. Free build shows only three top buttons (no brush), which fit in one row; its fresh paper button stays with the shapes, not in the top row. Build: paper sheet on the left, tray grid on the right (one to four columns so each cell stays at least 100 px); on portrait screens the tray sits under the sheet, with only as many rows as leave the sheet a fair share of the height. Pieces beyond those rows wait out of sight below the tray (not drawn, not reachable by mouse, keys or the helper hand) and slide up into the cells that free as pieces go in. Free build: sheet on the left, shapes in a grid on the right, the seven swatches in a row along the bottom; the fresh paper button takes the grid's last cell, which the seven shapes always leave free (two columns of four, three of three or four of two). Its radius is half the cell less 4 px, at least 48 px and at most the top buttons' radius. On portrait screens the sheet fills the width under the top row and the shapes plus one stepping swatch sit in a grid of 96 px cells along the bottom; the fresh paper button joins that grid when it needs no extra row, and otherwise stands beside the sheet's lower right corner, with the sheet narrowed to make room. Gallery: two shelves of six frames (three columns on portrait screens). Every target is at least 96 CSS px. Checked at 1366x768, 800x600 and 390x600; at 390x600 the build sheet is 262 px square beside the button column, with 100 px tray cells below it in at most two rows of three (one row at the first tier; with seven to ten pieces, six show and the rest wait below), and the free-build sheet is 264 x 198 px with the fresh paper button beside it, above the eight 96 px cells in two rows of four. The fresh paper button was also checked at 1920x1080 and at uiScale 0.75 and 2: at least 96 px across, inside the view, and clear of the sheet, the shape cells, the swatches and the top buttons.

## Voice clip slots

`src/games/shape-workshop/voice.ts` plays `public/voice/shape-workshop/<shape>.mp3` (circle, square, triangle, rectangle, star, heart, semicircle) when a piece of that shape lands, and when a shape is chosen in free build. On entry the game reads the hand-edited list `public/voice/shape-workshop/clips.json` (a JSON array of clip names) and only requests a clip whose name is in it, so adding a clip needs no code change and missing clips make no network request and no console error. A clip loads on its first request and plays only if, once loaded, it is still the latest request and the workshop is still the scene on top. Leaving the workshop or a scene covering it (the break nudge) drops every clip still loading and stops the one playing, and a new clip cuts off the one before it, so two clips never overlap. No clips ship; the list is `[]`. See `public/voice/shape-workshop/README.md`.

## Save data

`save.gameData('shape-workshop')`, per profile. The game definition registers `sanitize` (`src/games/shape-workshop/save.ts`) as `validateSave`, so the save store checks this bag at load, before every write and when it adopts another tab's data, even if the child never opens the workshop; the scene checks it again on entry. Malformed fields are reset and the stored document is protected; a missing field is filled with its default without protecting.

- `tier`, `motorStreak`, `level`, `learnStreak`: hidden difficulty.
- `demo`, `freeDemo`: whether each demonstration has played.
- `made`: times each picture was finished.
- `currentId`: the picture on the workbench. `wip`: per picture, its open part indices and the ones already placed, so switching pictures in the gallery keeps each half-built picture. Each also keeps `tally` (learning hits, learning misses, motor hits, motor misses, pointer placements, keyboard placements so far) and `tried` (open parts whose piece already tried a wrong outline), so a resumed picture adjusts difficulty and credits first tries as if it had never been left. A resumed picture deals one piece per empty open part, so when a piece goes into the outline of another piece of the same shape (the car's two wheels), the two pieces swap parts and their `tried` entries swap with them: the piece left in the tray keeps its own first-try state after leaving and coming back. Saves from before these fields load with zero counts and no tried parts.
- `sheets`: six free-build sheets, each a flat list of stamps (shape, colour, x, y, size, turn), at most 160 stamps per sheet; `lastSheet`. The fresh paper button empties the current sheet's list and writes it with `save.flush()` the moment it is pressed, before the sweep plays, so leaving during the sweep still finds the sheet empty; the sheet's gallery thumbnail is redrawn empty.
- `evidence`: per-shape learning hits and misses.

A finished picture is written with one `save.flush()` at the moment the last piece lands, before the celebration shows. A full sheet keeps every stamp; further stamps only sparkle with a soft tick, spaced like a normal trail: a still press sparkles once, a moving press once per stamp spacing.

## Art

Medium: layered cut paper. Shapes, outlines, the paper sheets, shelves and frames are drawn in code from point lists with a slightly torn white rim, a flat offset shadow layer and a generated paper grain multiplied inside the shape, all baked once into cached canvases on entry, resize or picture change. Generated bitmaps (see Provenance) supply the workshop background, the paper grain, the helper hand, the hub icon and the two mode-button icons. The fresh paper button's picture (a blank cream sheet with three teal swish marks and a yellow brush with a grey band and a brown handle) is drawn in code from the same paper shapes once per session, after the paper grain loads, and registered as a sprite, so the shared button code scales it and redraws it when the pixel ratio changes. The big sweeping brush (yellow bristles with bristle lines, a grey band, a brown handle) is baked from paper shapes with the free-build sheet, on entry, resize and pixel-ratio change.

## Performance plan

- The sheet with its glued parts and outlines is baked into one canvas on picture change, resize, and after each landing settles; a frame draws that canvas plus at most a few moving pieces.
- The blank sheet and the tray are rebaked only when their size changes. On a picture change the new parts and pieces are baked within a 3 ms budget per frame before the sheet slides in, so the change costs no frame over about 6 ms of work on the development machine.
- Each piece is a cached canvas per (shape, colour, size); free-build stamps use one cached canvas per shape and colour (warmed one per frame after entry) and are baked into the sheet layer once their slam finishes.
- When the engine's adaptive resolution changes the pixel ratio (same CSS size), the scene's resize rebakes every cached canvas at the new ratio: background, sheets, tray, parts, pieces, shelf, frames, effects, sheet layer and thumbnails. The stamp art records the ratio it was baked at and is dropped when that ratio is no longer in use, so stamps baked into the sheet after a rise in resolution are not soft.
- Gallery thumbnails are painted without extra canvases, one per frame and only when their picture or sheet changed, so opening the shelf with six full sheets costs at most about 8 ms in one frame on the development machine.
- The picture-done fanfare is rendered ahead once per session, as Bubble Bay does (`prepareSfxStep` in `src/audio/sfx.ts`): the first, longer step on entry under the enter fade (or at the next mode change if the sound was not ready yet), the short note steps in idle periods between frames, so the frame that plays it builds no notes.
- Sparkles, paper confetti and puffs live in fixed pools; no arrays, closures, strings, gradients, `shadowBlur` or `fillText` in update or render.
- The fresh paper sweep allocates nothing per frame: the sheet layer keeps the old stamps (live ones are baked into it when the button is pressed), and each frame draws the blank sheet, then only the part of the layer still ahead of the brush, then the three baked brush parts. Paper bits come from the effects pool.
- Work time is measured with `window.__corewise.loop.stats.workMean` and `workMax` at 1366x768. Entering free build with a full sheet (160 stamps) or resizing rebakes the sheet layer once, about 10 ms on the development machine.
