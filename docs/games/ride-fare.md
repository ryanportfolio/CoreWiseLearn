# Ride Fare

A round game about what coins are worth. Animal friends with little backpacks queue for a hot-air balloon ride. A brass fare box on the balloon basket shows the fare as a panel of round dot cups. The child drops coins from a wooden tray into the fare box; each coin lights as many cups as it is worth, one after another. When every cup is lit, the animal climbs into the basket, the balloon lifts off, drifts and lands again, and the next animal steps up. Learning tag: `counting` (the shared list of learning tags has no `money`; adding one is a change to shared code and needs the owner's OK).

Nothing is bought or kept. The child helps someone else ride; the tray refills every round; there is no wallet, balance, shop or goal that spans sessions, and coins never touch stars or stickers (`docs/research/07-money.md`, section 2, "Commercial framing"). The selected concept is `docs/games/ride-fare/concept.webp`.

## The action and its response

- **Pay a coin.** Press a coin on the tray and carry it to the fare box: drag it there and let go, or click the coin and then click the fare box (the coin follows the pointer between the two clicks). At motor tier 0 a click (a press released within 0.3 s and 24 px) on a coin sends it straight into the slot; a drag still carries it. A coin released within the snap distance of the fare box counts as dropped on it.
- **Each coin pays at once:** the coin flies to the slot (0.3 s), shows its own dots for 0.25 s as a ring of glowing dots on the coin (one dot for a penny, five for a nickel, ten for a dime), then turns edge-on and slips in with a clink (0.15 s). Its value then pours into the cups: a penny lights the next cup, a nickel lights five cups one after another, 70 ms apart (all five within 0.3 s; measured 260 to 272 ms), a dime ten in one quicker pour, 32 ms apart (about 0.3 s). Each lit cup glows warm yellow with a soft pitched tick that climbs through each group of ten, so a nickel is heard as five quick steps and a dime as a run of ten.
- **Too much:** a coin worth more than the unlit cups touches the slot, shows its dots for a moment, and hops back to its place on the tray with a soft giggle sound while the animal smiles. Nothing is lost and no cup changes. The same gentle hop brings back a coin the swap stand or the paws do not take (steps 7 and 8).
- **Fare paid:** when the last cup lights, the cups pulse once together (0.35 s), the animal hops into the basket (0.5 s; its waving pose, clipped in code at the basket's front rim line so it stands inside) and the balloon rises off the top of the screen with it (0.7 s). A fresh balloon then lands (0.7 s) while the small balloon carries the rider up and away across the sky in the background (2.6 s, fading at the end). The next animal walks up to the gate while the balloon lifts, its coins slide onto the tray while the new balloon lands, and it can pay as soon as both are done: 2.27 s from the last cup lit to the next rider ready (measured over three step-3 riders; round 2 took about 6 s). During the lift and landing the tray rests; presses then make the animals hop.
- **The animals** are content, curious or delighted. None is ever shown waiting impatiently, sad or pleading.

## Discovery without words

The very first round shows the goal first: the fare box with five cups already lit (held 0.5 s), the hedgehog climbing in and the balloon lifting off (about 2 s in all). A fresh balloon lands while the hedgehog drifts away small, and the first rider (the bunny) steps up with a fare of 6 and a tray with a nickel and five pennies. While the coins are still sliding in, the watercolour helper hand rises from the bottom edge, presses the nickel, carries it to the slot and lets go; the five dots pour out and five cups light. The first cup lights 4.5 s after the game opens (measured 4473 ms; round 2 took about 7 s). The hand then rests on the next penny and taps it in a loop until the child does anything, so the child finishes the fare with one penny. The introduction's other two riders come from the child's current step. The introduction records no evidence, changes no motor tier and always earns three stars.

Idle: after 6 quiet seconds the hand carries a hint coin (see-through, in a pulsing warm halo, never paying anything) from a coin that helps now to where it goes, then fades; it repeats every 7 s while the child stays idle. The hint picks the largest coin the fare box takes; at step 7, when the box takes nothing on the tray, a coin for the swap stand; at step 8 a penny for the paws. A hinted fare still counts as paid but records no learning evidence.

**First-time demonstrations of the age-6 ideas.** The first rider whose content is step 5, 6, 7 or 8 for a profile gets one ghost-hand demonstration of that step's new idea; the save remembers which ones the profile has seen (`demos`), so each plays once per profile. The demonstrated rider records no evidence.

- Step 5: the hand carries a dime to the slot and ten cups pour, a whole panel; the child pays the rest.
- Step 6: the hand carries a dime and a whole row of ten small cups lights while the counter under the numeral reads 10¢.
- Step 7: the rider's plate asks for a nickel and the tray holds five pennies. The hand carries the pennies to the swap stand one by one (about 1 s each), they slide together into a nickel with five dots inside, the nickel hops onto the tray, and the hand then taps it until the child acts.
- Step 8: the animal pays its dime itself, the change cups glow copper, the hand hands one penny back to the paws, then taps the pennies until the child acts.

## Round flow and attempts

1. **Riders:** 3 animals per round at motor tier 0, 4 at tiers 1 and 2. Six animals (hedgehog, bunny, fox, raccoon, bear, mouse) take turns; the queue on the path shows the round's next riders (up to three), so progress is visible as fewer animals waiting. The first rider of a round above step 2 comes from the step below (a warm-up), the rest from the current step. At step 8, the top, a round's riders come from steps 5, 8, 6 and 8 in that order.
2. **Celebration:** the balloon floats across the meadow with every rider of the round waving from the basket, confetti, stars land one by one. Input is ignored for the first 1.5 s and until the last star lands; it ends by itself at 4.6 s.
3. **Sticker choice:** while this game's six stickers are not all owned and `rewardsEnabled` is true, the shared `createStickerOffers` helper (`src/ui/sticker-offer.ts`) shows two stickers, each on a soft cream disc like the clouds, with the small sticker book beside them; its `warm` and `warmBook` run during the celebration. Same guard as every game: input ignored for 1.2 s, nothing focused, the first key only shows focus.
4. **Still rest:** the basket on the meadow, the sticker book with the new sticker (or the round's riders waving when there is no gift), the stars, and Again and Home of equal size and colour. `services.roundBoundary()` runs when rest is reached.

Stars: every finished round earns 3. Bounced coins never cost a star. Awards persist once, as in Bubble Bay and Dino Picnic: round count, stars and the offered pair go into the game bag's `pending` field with a unique round `id` and `save.flush()` before the celebration shows; leaving or reloading returns to the choice or the rest; leaving rest by any route clears `pending`. The tray, the fares and every coin are play material only and are never saved.

**Attempts (motor):** one carry counts once, whatever the input style. Releasing a carried coin on a target (the fare box, the swap stand at step 7 or the paws at step 8, each with the tier's snap distance) is one hit; releasing it anywhere off the tray is one miss and the coin slides back to its place (released over the tray, it just goes back). A press on empty play space is one miss. A coin that bounces back because it was worth too much (or the stand or paws did not take it) is still a hit: it reached the target. Picking up, key presses, the tier-0 one-press send, demonstrations and hints never count. At the end of a round: 8 or more attempts with under 70 percent hits moves down a tier; 12 or more at 90 percent or better is a qualifying round, and two in a row move up a tier.

When there are more coins than places, coins of one kind share a place as a stack (the top coin is the target, the coins below show as offset rims, up to five; the stack refills from below when its supply is unlimited). - **Tray places** are the hit targets for coins: the place's full width (at least 96 CSS px: the nickel's diameter plus 16u) by the tray row's height (the nickel's diameter plus 36u), so a coin is pressed anywhere in its part of the tray.

## Learning steps (hidden, separate from motor)

Learning evidence is one result per rider of the current step (not the warm-up rider) whose fare the child paid with deliberate pointer choices: a coin counts as deliberate when the press that picked it (or, at tier 0, sent it) came at least 0.7 s after the child's previous press in play, so a stream of quick presses plays fully but counts nothing (choosing which coin is the skill in every step, so tier 0 records evidence too). Riders paid with any key press, after a hint, or in a demonstration record nothing. "Exact" means no coin bounced back while paying that rider (at step 8, no extra penny handed back) and, at steps 3 to 6, that the payment used the step's coin (table below). A fare paid in pennies alone at steps 3 to 6 still rides with the full sequence; it just counts as not exact, so it eases the step like a bounce does and never moves the child up.

Between rounds, at the current step: if every counted rider of the round was exact and there were at least 3, move up one step; otherwise 6 or more exact riders in the step's last 8 counted move up, and 2 or fewer in the last 6 move down one step (never below step 1). Exposure steps (2 and 7) move up after 2 finished rounds at that step whatever happened. A step change resets that step's window. A child who plays only with keys records no evidence, so after 3 finished rounds at step 1 or 2 with no counted rider the step moves up by one, up to step 3; beyond that only evidence moves it.

At step 8 the top of the progression is reached: rounds there mix riders from steps 5, 6 and 8 (only the step-8 riders count as evidence), so the 6-year-old keeps getting varied full rounds. Two or fewer exact step-8 riders in the last six move the child back to step 7, as at every other step.

The swap stand never strands a child. It takes pennies while the plate still wants its coin and it holds pennies or nothing; it takes a nickel only when the plate wants a dime and no penny is left anywhere (tray, hand, flights or stand), so a lone nickel and some pennies can never block each other on it. A coin it does not take hops back. Its dotted circles show only while it would take a coin; once the coin the plate asks for is made, the stand takes nothing more and its circles go. Swapped coins and anything left on the stand go when the rider leaves: nothing is bought or kept.

**Tier 0 at steps 7 and 8.** Those steps have a second target besides the fare box (the swap stand at step 7, the animal's paws at step 8), so the single press needs a rule for where the coin goes. At tier 0 one press on a coin sends it to the target the step asks for next: at step 8 to the paws, the only target while change is owed; at step 7 to the fare box when the box takes that coin, else to the swap stand when the stand takes it, else to the fare box (where it hops back). Drag and click-then-target still reach either target. The paws target is one rectangle over the animal's hands and lower body, 160 x 120 CSS px at tier 0, 140 x 110 at tier 1 and 120 x 100 at tier 2 at 1366x768 (those numbers times `u`, never under 96 CSS px on either side at any window size), with the tier's snap distance around it, clear of the fare-box zone and the tray.

**Easing and no stall.** Hidden difficulty never waits for "a nickel counts as five": every fare can always be paid with what is on the tray (a coin that is too much just bounces, and when the dimes, nickels and pennies left can no longer make the unlit cups exactly, pennies are added to the penny place, where a stack that never runs out counts as any number of its coins, so a step-6 tray keeps five pennies however large the fare; at step 7 the stand rule above keeps every swap reachable; at step 8 the pennies never run out), so every round finishes. A child who struggles at any step from 3 to 8 moves back one step and gets full rounds of that content (above step 2 each round opens with a warm-up rider from the step below). Steps 1 to 3 together are a complete game for a 4-year-old: matching coin pictures, filling penny cups, and seeing the nickel pour five. Struggling inside a round only brings the idle hint sooner (after 4 s instead of 6 s once two coins have bounced on the same rider); the content never changes mid-round.

## Mistake handling

There is no wrong state. A coin that is too much, does not match a step-1 or step-7 picture, is not taken by the swap stand, or is one penny too many for the paws, hops back to its tray place with a soft giggle (three quiet `pop` A notes) and the animal smiles; no error sound, no red, no shake. A coin dropped on empty space slides back to its place with a `whoosh`. Coins can never be lost: any coin not in the slot always returns to the tray. Repeated presses while a coin is still pouring send nothing more; the press shows a small hop of the pressed coin.

## Keyboard-only and mouse-only play

- **Mouse or trackpad only:** every action is a press, a drag or a click-click; any mouse button counts. The context menu is cancelled.
- **Keyboard only:** a bobbing arrow and a warm ring mark the highlighted coin on the tray; it starts on a coin that fits the fare (or the leftmost). Left and Right (Up and Down step the same way) move between every coin on the tray, including one worth more than the unlit cups; the highlight stays where the child put it and moves on only when that place runs out. Any other key sends the highlighted coin into the slot (at most one every 150 ms, and none while a coin is still on its way in or pouring: the highlighted coin hops instead); a coin that is too much hops back as usual, and the highlight then moves to a coin that helps (the largest the box takes; at step 7 one the stand takes; at step 8 a penny), so a child who only presses one key still finishes every fare. At step 7 the swap stand is a second target: the first key lifts the coin over the target the tier-0 rule picks (with a warm ring round that target), arrows switch between the stand and the box, and the next key drops. At step 8 the same applies with the animal's paws. A coin lifted with a key stays a key carry even when the mouse then drops it on a target: it is never a motor attempt, and the rider records no learning evidence (lifting it already counts as a key press). During play every key plays, Escape, Tab and Enter included. After the round, Escape goes home and Tab reaches the corner Home and sound buttons; choice and rest follow the shared rule (first key shows focus, arrows move, the next key chooses). Keys never record learning evidence.

## Layout

The background `launch-field.webp` cover-fits the window. Pieces are placed in layout units `u`, here `min(width / 1366, height / 768)` kept between 0.45 and 1.405 (so nothing draws above its own pixels at 1920x1080), times the config `uiScale`. Measured once from the sprites' pixels: the fare box's dark window spans 0.265 to 0.912 of its width and 0.271 to 0.859 of its height (the cups and the step-1 plate are drawn there), its slot is centred at 0.146 of the width with its top at 0.37 of the height; the basket's rim top is at 0.379 of its height, the rim's lower edge at 0.476, and animals inside are clipped at the front rim line, 0.425.

- **Tray:** along the bottom edge, 20u in from each side (at least 6 px), its bottom 12u above the window's bottom (at least 6 px). Each row is the nickel's diameter plus 36u tall: 178 px at tier 0, 164 at tier 1, 150 at tier 2 at 1366x768. Three places hold any rider's coins, so where fewer than three fit in one row the tray gets as many rows (16u apart) as three places need. The places are spread evenly around the tray's centre. When a resize leaves fewer places than the tray is using, coins of one kind stack: the last place of the kind with the most places joins that kind's first place (counts, an endless supply and swapped-coin dots carry over) until the tray fits. ## Performance

No allocation in update or render: coins in flight (24) and particles (160) are pooled, and the coin dots are one baked glow drawn per dot. Every sprite is drawn at a fixed size per layout and animated with transforms, so one layout keeps one cached scaled canvas per sprite size; the scaled-canvas cache is released when the canvas size, pixel ratio, tier or fit changes, and on exit. The unlit, lit and copper change cup (in the grid size and the smaller step-6 size), the coin-dot glow, the hint halo, the dashed rings for the swap stand and the empty tray places, and two glyph strips "0123456789¢" (dark for the fare, orange for the counter, Andika at 0.42 of the plate height) are baked on CPU canvases (`willReadFrequently: true`, see pitfalls) when their size changes, never per frame; a numeral is drawn by copying glyphs from its strip at whole pixels. The step-6 pieces are baked only in rounds at step 5 or above. The tray is baked once per size: two end caps at one scale and the middle repeated in an odd number of copies, every other one mirrored, so each copy is drawn at 1.0 or less and both ends meet their caps as in the source. The background is rescaled only when the canvas size or pixel ratio changes. No gradients, `shadowBlur` or `fillText` per frame. Animals inside the basket are clipped with one rectangle `clip()` per frame (save and restore). The celebration, choice and rest art is scaled ahead in idle periods during play, one sprite per idle period, after the fanfare is prepared a step at a time. An idle callback runs a step only with 4 ms to spare, except that a callback that timed out, or a step that has waited 500 ms over short idle periods, runs one step anyway (the rule in `src/ui/sticker-offer.ts` and Piggy Parade), so a machine that is never idle still finishes the preparation. Measured at 1366x768 on the dev box (round 3, whole mouse rounds at steps 6, 7 and 8 from opening the game through the celebration): scene `workMean` 0.14 to 0.18 ms, `workMax` 0.7 to 1.0 ms over play, and 4.5 to 4.7 ms in the first 2.5 s after opening (the bakes); loop `workMean` 0.19 to 0.22 ms. Delivered frames: mean interval 10.0 ms (100 Hz panel), 99th percentile 10.1 to 10.2 ms, one to three frames of 30 to 40 ms in the first 150 ms after the game opens (first draws of the scaled art), none over 50 ms. Round 2: `workMean` 0.15 to 0.19 ms, `workMax` 1.4 to 2.0 ms.

## Art

Medium: storybook watercolour in the concept's golden late-afternoon light, true-colour WebP. Shared controls (Home, sound, play arrow) reuse the existing art. Everything counted or exact is drawn in code: cups and their lit state, dots inside and around coins, the dotted swap circles, the coin plate and the numerals with ¢. No image contains text, numerals or a group of things to count.

All bitmaps were generated on 2026-10-04 with the built-in `image_gen` tool in Codex CLI 0.159.0 (`codex exec -m gpt-6-astra -c model_reasoning_effort=medium`). The tool did not report the image model. Every request attached the selected concept (`r3.png`, 1672x941) as its first reference and shared one style block: soft storybook watercolour with gentle painterly edges, fine warm brown linework, rich warm colours and golden light from the right, matching the concept's shapes, proportions and palette, with no text, letters, numbers, logos, inscriptions or interface. Sprite requests asked for a transparent background; every result came back with a clean alpha channel (objects about 253, surroundings 0).

Processing (`sharp`, scripts kept with the sources): regions of alpha above 128 were found and isolated, alpha under 16 cleared and over 235 made solid, colours at soft edges replaced by nearby solid colours (no dark or light fringe), specks under 300 px removed, cropped to the alpha bounds with 4 px padding, resized with Lanczos 3 and written as WebP quality 80, alpha quality 50. The two poses of each animal share one scale (waiting pose 480 px tall) so they swap without the animal changing size; both are anchored at the feet. Coins: each face's circle was fitted from its alpha area and centroid, cropped 2 px inside it, resized to 384x384 and given an exact anti-aliased round alpha edge, so the coin fills its square and code scales it to the true ratios. Each file was checked on mid-grey, dark and light grounds at 1:1 for halos and stray pixels (contact sheet in the sources folder).

`launch-field.webp` follows the owner's three-strip method from PR #13: the 1536x1024 `bg-base.png` was cut into three overlapping 683x1024 portrait strips, each enlarged to 1024x1536 (Lanczos 3) and attached with the concept to its own request asking for a sharper, faithful redraw of exactly that strip. The PR #13 stitch script placed each strip where it best matched the base enlarged 1.5 times (alignment error 10 to 14), replaced each strip's broad colour (Gaussian sigma 32 px) with the base's, joined neighbours along the vertical path of least difference (6 px feather) and resized the 2304x1536 mosaic to 1920x1280 (Lanczos 3); WebP quality 80, opaque. Round 2 (2026-10-04) fixed a visible join in the bottom grass near x 1190, y 880 to 1280, where the middle strip's grass is smoother than the right strip's: the same raw strips were stitched again with the same script, except that below mosaic row 1150 (ramping in over 100 rows) the right-hand join blends across the strips' whole overlap (mosaic x 1310 to 1630, smoothstep) instead of the 6 px feather. No new generation; the script is `stitch2.mjs` in the sources folder. 1:1 crops before and after: `D:/screenshots/CoreWiseLearn/games/ride-fare/r2/seam-before-1to1.png` and `seam-after-1to1.png`. It shows only the sky, mountains, lake, village, the empty meadow on the left, the empty path and fence on the right and plain grass along the bottom: no balloon, basket, animals, coins or tray.
