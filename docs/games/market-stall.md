# Market Stall

A round game about giving change. The child is the cashier of a bread stall on a harbour, seen from behind the counter. Animal customers come to the counter one at a time with something to buy: a loaf, a pretzel, a pie. Each item has a price tag, and the customer always pays more than the price into the wooden dish on the counter. The child counts the change back from the till into the customer's open paw, counting up from the price to the payment (7¢ paid with a dime: 8, 9, 10; 75¢ paid with $2: 80¢, 90¢, $1.00, $2.00). Then the customer takes the item and the change and leaves happily. It covers Common Core 2.MD.C.8 (money word problems with $ and ¢). Learning tag: `counting` (the shared list of learning tags has no `money`; adding one is a change to shared code and needs the owner's OK).

Nothing is bought or kept by the child. The child is the seller and serves someone else; the till refills every round; there is no wallet, balance, shop or goal that spans sessions, and money never touches stars or stickers (`docs/research/07-money.md` section 2; `docs/research/08-money-grades-1-2.md` section 6, "Shared rules"). The selected concept is `docs/games/market-stall/concept.webp` (the owner's pick, MS2, 2026-10-05).

**The cashier game (round RD1, 2026-10-06).** After playing the first version, the owner said: "I don't get this game. you just click the object? where's the counting / learning / challenge come in?" and "im thinking the user is the cashier. and the animal is buying something, it gives you lets say $2, and if it costs 75c then what's their change etc. That example would be more advanced so it can start with easier ones like if it costs 10c and I give you 25c etc". So every customer now pays more than the price and every customer needs change. The "enough, or more please" decision, the customer's purse and the steps that only decided are gone. The owner also allowed decimal points (see "Currency illustrations").

## The action and its response

- **Count out the change.** The till holds coins in round wells (or bills in long slots; a dollars-and-cents till has both). Carry a piece to the customer's open paw in either of two ways, at every motor tier and with any mouse button: drag it there with the button held and let go, or click it (press and release without moving 24 px) and it follows the pointer with no button held until the next click, which puts it down. Put down on the paw zone (or within the tier's snap distance of it) it pays; put down anywhere else it hops gently back to its well. While carried the piece is drawn lifted (1.12 of its size, 12u higher, over a baked soft shadow), a faint copy stays in its well so the well still shows its kind and value, and the paw's warm glow grows. (Owner, 2026-10-05: click to carry everywhere in the hub, one behaviour.)
- **Each piece of change pays at once:** the piece flies to the paw (0.3 s) and joins a compact stack there at full size, drawn over the till: the newest piece in the paw zone (its lower edge never below the zone's), each of the four newest 0.12 of a coin up and to the left of the one after it (bills step half as far sideways), so every piece shows an edge. From the fifth piece on, the older pieces settle in a column just left of those four, oldest at the bottom, each 0.1 of a piece above the one before (closing up so the column rises at most 0.45 of a piece), under a cream card with an ink edge showing their value ("91¢", "$6", "$1.25") in the tags' numerals. The paw draws up to 100 pieces (95 pennies at step 5 fit); past that, which only a child giving hundreds of pennies at steps 8 and 9 can reach, the oldest pieces' value moves onto the card, so the fan and the card always add up to the change given. The stack keeps clear of the till: its bounds cover every kind the till holds (a coin with its value tag, which for a quarter is taller than a bill), in each of the fan's four places, and the settled column; a stack whose bounds would reach the till first slides left (while the newest piece stays over the paw zone), else rises above the till (round RD2: a quarter's tag in a dollars-and-cents paw crossed the till's rim by 13.8 px at 1366x768, tier 1, the pelican's step-8 demonstration). The counter numeral on the board climbs by the piece's value, one step per cent (or per dollar at the $ steps) with a soft rising tick (a penny one step; a nickel five steps 60 ms apart; a dime ten steps 30 ms apart; a quarter 25 steps 14 ms apart; a $1 bill in cents 100 steps 3 ms apart, so every pour takes about 0.3 s).
- **Too much:** a coin or bill worth more than the change still owed touches the paw, jiggles there for 0.9 s with its value shown just above it as solid deep-ink dots on a cream card with an ink edge (dots about 0.13 of a penny across, at least 12 px; rows of ten split 5 and 5, so a quarter shows two rows of ten and a five, a $20 bill two rows of ten), and hops back to its well (0.5 s) with a soft giggle while the customer smiles. A $1 bill in cents (100 dots would not fit a card) jiggles and hops back with no dots. Nothing changes on the board.
- **Done:** when the counter reaches the payment, the counter (and at steps 1 to 3 the cups) pulses once (0.35 s), the customer switches to its happy pose, the item glides into its arms (0.4 s), and it walks off to the right with the change and the item (0.8 s). The change total is said as it leaves, once any coin or bill name still being said has finished; the next customer waits for it. The next customer walks in from the left (0.6 s), sets the item down, the tag swings, and the payment drops from its paw into the dish (coins 0.35 s each, one after another 0.12 s apart; a bill flutters down in 0.5 s, turning a little but always at its full size, and no dish piece is ever a target). The payment then pours onto the board, largest piece first, each piece hopping as it pours: the payment line's amount climbs (and at steps 1 to 3 its cups light), about 0.3 s a piece, a $1 or $5 bill in cents in 1.2 s. The till slides up from the bottom edge during the drop and pour (0.35 s). The child can act as soon as the pour ends.
- **The dish** shows the payment. Bills draw at full size and fan from left to right, each covering the right part of the one before (0.38 of a width apart and never closer, since a bill's numeral ends at 0.37 of its width); a lower row of bills sits 0.74 of a bill below the row above, under that bill's numeral. Coins draw at the smallest tier's size (dime 96u, never under 96 px) and sit side by side in one row, one slot apart (the wider of the coin and its value tag, plus 4u), so no coin covers another coin's tag; a row wider than the dish reaches past its rim, up to 0.6 of the window (only a row wider than that would take a second row, below the first row's tags; no payment needs one). Kinds follow largest first; bills take at most two rows (three only when two cannot fit the widest dish). The dish never grows taller for its pile and never reaches above the counter's back edge, so it never covers the customer or a price tag; a pile that needs more height reaches past the dish's rim (that footprint counts as the dish's in the layout).
- **The customers** are content, curious or delighted. None is ever shown waiting impatiently, sad, pleading or disappointed.

## Discovery without words

The very first round shows the goal first: the otter walks in (0.6 s), the tag says 7¢, a dime is already in the dish (the board shows 7¢, the payment line 10¢, and ten cups: 7 yellow, 3 orange-red change cups), three pennies hop from the till into its paw 0.5 s apart while the counter climbs 8, 9, 10, and the otter waves and walks off with its loaf (about 3.3 s in all; presses meanwhile make the otter hop). Then the heron steps up with a 6¢ pretzel and a dime; once the dime has poured and the till has slid up, the linocut helper hand rises from the bottom edge, presses a penny in the till, carries it to the heron's paw and lets go; the counter climbs to 7. The hand then rests on the well of the next helpful piece (the penny well here) and taps it in a loop until the child does anything; the keyboard highlight moves to the same well, so the two cues agree. The child finishes the change with three pennies (8, 9, 10). The introduction's other customers come from the child's current step. The introduction records no evidence, changes no motor tier and always earns three stars.

Idle: after 6 quiet seconds the hand shows the next helpful piece: it carries a see-through hint piece (in a pulsing warm halo, paying nothing) from that piece's well to the paw, then fades, and repeats every 7 s while the child stays idle; the keyboard highlight moves to that well. Counting up, as every demonstration teaches: ones (pennies or $1 bills) to the next 5, a five (a nickel or $5 bill) to the next 10, then tens (dimes or $10 bills), and in dollars and cents a $1 bill at every whole dollar while a dollar or more is owed; when that kind is not in the till or is too much, the largest smaller of those that fits, else the largest that fits (`nextPiece` in `rules.ts`). A customer finished after a hint still leaves happily but records no learning evidence. After two bounces on one customer, the hint comes after 4 s instead of 6 s (08 section 6, "Easing after misses").

**First-time demonstrations.** The first customer whose content is a new step for a profile gets one ghost-hand demonstration of that step's new idea; the save remembers which demonstrations the profile has seen (`cashDemos`: bit n for step n's idea), so each plays once per profile. The introduction's taught customer counts as step 1's demonstration. A warm-up customer gets its content's demonstration too. The demonstrated customer records no evidence. Steps 2, 4, 7 and 8 show theirs on a customer with fixed numbers.

- Step 1: as in the introduction (one penny to the paw, the child finishes).
- Step 2: the first step-2 customer is always 13¢ paid with a quarter: the hand moves a penny (14), a penny (15), then a nickel (20), and taps the nickel well; the child gives the last nickel (25).
- Step 3: the hand carries one penny from the till to the paw (26¢ paid 30¢: 27); the child finishes.
- Step 4: the first step-4 customer is always 23¢ paid with two quarters: the hand moves a penny (24), a penny (25), then a nickel (30), and taps the dime well; the child finishes with two dimes (40, 50).
- Step 5: the $1 bill drops into the dish and the payment line climbs to $1.00; the hand moves the first coin of counting up (a penny to the next 5, a nickel to the next 10, else a dime).
- Step 6: the hand carries one $1 bill from the till to the paw; the counter climbs from the price by $1.
- Step 7: the first step-7 customer is always $13 paid with $20: the hand moves a $1 bill (14), another (15), and taps the $5 slot; the child gives the $5 (20).
- Step 8: the first step-8 customer is always 75¢ paid with two $1 bills (the owner's example): the hand moves a nickel (80¢), a dime (90¢), a dime ($1.00), and taps the $1 slot; the child gives the $1 bill ($2.00).
- Step 9: first the two tags slide together into one tag showing the total, and the board shows the total the moment they meet (with a small pop and chips); then the payment drops (1.3 s in all, the tags taking the first 0.45 of it; the first time 2.1 s, with the hand following the second tag into the first).
- After every carry demonstration the hand taps the well of the next helpful piece, and the keyboard highlight moves there too.

## Round structure and attempts

1. **Customers:** every round counts at least three customers (so the clean-round rule can always fire): at step 1, 3 at motor tier 0 and 4 at tiers 1 and 2; above step 1 the warm-up customer comes on top, so 4 at every tier (the introduction: the goal, then 3). Six customers (heron, otter, pelican, puffin, seal, bear) take turns in a fixed rotation that continues across rounds (`turn` in the save); each brings one of eight goods (round loaf, long loaf, braided loaf, pretzel, fish biscuit, pie, honey jar, cake), never the same good twice in a row. Above step 1 the first customer of a round comes from the step below (a warm-up that records nothing); the rest come from the current step. At step 9, the top, the warm-up comes from step 7 and step 8 in turn, round by round, and the other three customers are step-9 customers, so every round counts three and still varies.
2. **Closing the till** (see "Round end" below): the day's takings are counted and buy something for the harbour.
3. **Celebration:** the round's customers stand in a row behind the counter (clipped at its back edge, half the window tall at most), each in its happy pose with its good in front, bobbing and waving, the row kept within the middle 56 percent of the window so the harbour's change stays in view; linocut confetti (carved chips in the four inks) bursts from the middle and two flocks of gulls lift off; the lighthouse lamp, when it was this round's change, glows gently; stars land one by one. Input is ignored for the first 1.5 s and until the last star lands (1.56 s for three stars); it ends by itself at 4.6 s.
4. **Sticker choice:** while this game's six stickers are not all owned and `rewardsEnabled` is true, the shared `createStickerOffers` helper (`src/ui/sticker-offer.ts`, reused, not copied) shows two stickers standing on the counter, with the small sticker book beside them; its `warm` and `warmBook` run during the celebration. Same guard as every game: input ignored for 1.2 s, nothing focused, the first key only shows focus.
5. **Still rest:** the empty stall with the harbour (its bunting and this round's change still up, all still), the sticker book with the new sticker (or the round's customers when there is no gift, standing still: no bobbing or swaying at rest), the stars, and Again and Home of equal size and colour (mustard circles, 62u radius, at least 48 px). `services.roundBoundary()` runs when rest is reached.

Stars: every finished round earns 3. Bounced pieces never cost a star. Awards persist once, as in Ride Fare and Bubble Bay: round count, stars and the offered pair go into the game bag's `pending` field with a unique round `id` and `save.flush()` when the last customer leaves, before the till count and the celebration show; leaving or reloading returns to the choice or the rest; leaving rest by any route clears `pending`. The till, the dish, every payment, every coin and bill, the till count, the harbour change, the bunting and the crowd are play material only and are never saved; the next round starts with none of them.

**Attempts (motor):** one carry (by drag or by click to carry) counts once, whatever the input style. Releasing a carried coin or bill on the paw zone (with the tier's snap distance) is one hit; releasing it anywhere off the till is one miss and it slides back to its well (released over the till, it just goes back). A piece that bounces back because it was too much still reached the target and is a hit. Picking up, key presses, demonstrations and hints never count. At the end of a round: 8 or more attempts with under 70 percent hits moves down a tier; 12 or more at 90 percent or better is a qualifying round, and two in a row move up a tier (Ride Fare's rule).

- **Bills** are 2:1. Their short side never draws below 96 CSS px (so never under 192 x 96), and at 1366x768 they are at most 240 x 120 CSS px, inside the 280 x 140 cap of 08 section 5. Every bill draws at its full tier size wherever it appears (dish, till, paw, in flight); a carried piece draws at 1.12 of it, and up to 1.28 wide for an instant in its pick-up squash.
- **Every zone is at least 96 CSS px on both sides** at every window size (the u-sized numbers above are floored at 96). At 1366x768 and 1920x1080 zones never overlap each other, the board, the dish (while it shows), a corner button or the window's edge (see "Layout"); the layout checks this for every customer and tier at those two sizes. Smaller windows use the same rules but are not verified and may overlap (owner decision, under "Layout").
- **The till's distractor kinds** are the only motor change in content: a "more" kind is the next larger coin (or bill) the change does not need, falling back to the next smaller; it can still be used where it fits (any correct change counts), and bounces where it does not. They never change the learning step's numbers.

## Learning steps (hidden, separate from motor)

Learning evidence is one result per customer of the current step (not the warm-up customer, not a demonstrated or hinted one). A piece counts as deliberate when the press that picked it up came at least 0.7 s after the child's previous press in play; with keys, when the child moved the highlight with an arrow key and waited at least 0.4 s before the action key (08 section 6, "Shared rules"). An arrow pressed for one customer never counts for the next: a new customer starts with no arrow pressed. A customer any of whose change pieces was not deliberate records nothing, so a stream of quick presses plays fully but counts nothing. Counted right: the change exact with no piece bounced.

Amounts counted in cents are written in ¢ under $1 ("75¢") and with a decimal point from $1 up ("$1.25", "$2.00"); whole-dollar amounts at steps 6, 7 and 9 are written "$7". A strong 6-year-old can climb from step 1: steps 1 and 3 need only counting on by ones (Ride Fare already hands back 1 to 9 pennies from a dime), step 2 adds nickels and counting past 5 and 10, and every later step adds one idea.

**Moving between steps** (between rounds only; 08 section 6, copied from Ride Fare): at the current step, move up one step when every counted customer of the round was right and there were at least 3, or when 6 of the step's last 8 counted customers were right; move down one step when 2 or fewer of the last 6 were right (never below step 1). A step change resets that step's window. Every round above step 1 opens with one warm-up customer from the step below.

**Easing and no stall.** Every task can always be finished: the till's wells never run out (each well shows a stack of four pieces with offset rims and refills from below), a coin or bill that is too much just bounces, and the change can always be finished. Two rules make sure of it (round RD2). Every till holds the unit piece: pennies at the ¢ steps (step 8's and step 9's dollars-and-cents tills included) and $1 bills at the $ steps, so any amount still owed can be paid. And the scene takes a piece only when it is no more than the change still owed and what is left after it can still be paid from the till (`accepts` in `rules.ts`); with the unit piece always there, this never turns away a piece that is not too much. Before round RD2 a $1.70 item paid with $2 got a till of dimes and quarters at tier 1 (step 8's till had no pennies), and a quarter, accepted at 30¢ owed, left 5¢ that nothing in the till could pay.

**The exhaustive change check** (`window.__marketStallChangeCheck()` on the dev page `dev/market-stall.html?debug`, in `src/dev/market-stall.ts`): for every step 1 to 9 and tier 0 to 2, 4,000 seeded customers from `planCustomer` plus every fixed customer (the introduction's two, the step-2, step-4, step-7 and step-8 demonstrations), each kept once, and for each one every amount still owed that some sequence of accepted pieces can reach (the amount owed is the whole state, so this covers every order of pieces). Round RD2 result: 6,910 distinct customers, 433,831 reachable amounts, zero dead ends, zero pieces turned away that were not too much, every till with its unit piece; every price of every step's range was drawn at every tier (steps 1 to 8 all of theirs, step 9 all 131 totals). As a control, the old rule with pennies taken out of step 8's tills gets stuck on 30 of its 210 customers (`D:/screenshots/CoreWiseLearn/market-stall/rd2/change-check.json`). A child who struggles at a step moves back one step and gets full rounds of that content. A keyboard-only child who never uses the arrows records no evidence; after 3 finished rounds at step 1 or 2 with no counted customer, the step moves up by one, up to step 3; beyond that only evidence moves it. Struggling inside a round only brings the idle hint sooner; the content never changes mid-round.

## Feel and feedback

Owner, 2026-10-05: "what keeps people engaged is the visuals, the sounds, the feedback, so lets go all out on that aspect", and "make sure its fun and enjoyable to learn about money and wanting to earn it". Round 4 added this layer. Every effect is warm and calm: nothing flashes, nothing is shrill, no screen shake, nothing waits for an effect to finish (every effect runs alongside play), and none is a reward for being right (a bounced piece lands with the same feel).

- **Pick-up:** the piece squashes wide (1.28 by 0.99 of its size) and springs back to its lifted 1.12 over 0.35 s, rising 12u above the pointer over a baked soft ink shadow; it sways a little with the pointer's speed. Two cream glints at its well; coins play a quiet `pop` B, bills `paper-rustle`.
- **Flight to the paw:** an arc (0.3 s); a coin turns over once (its face flips to the back and to the front again as it turns; its value tag turns with it and hides while the back shows), a bill flutters.
- **Landing:** the newest piece in the paw squashes and settles (0.3 s); a `coin-stack` clink whose pitch climbs with the stack (bills: `paper-rustle`); six carved chips and a glint; a coin's chips are its own metal (copper for a penny, silver for the others) with cream, a bill's are the four inks.
- **Payment into the dish:** each piece lands with a hop, three small chips (a coin's in its metal with cream, a bill's orange-red, mustard and cream) and a `coin-stack` clink climbing piece by piece (bills: `paper-rustle`).
- **Cups:** each cup pops in coming down from 1.35 of its size, dipping just under and settling (0.3 s), with the rising `tick` as before.
- **The till** rises with a small overshoot, like a drawer popping open, with `till-drawer`.
- **Happy customers, more each time:** when a customer is paid it cheers, and the cheer grows with each customer of the round: the first hops once; the second hops twice with a wave (a sway); the third hops three times with a squash on each landing; the fourth dances four hops. Carved chips burst from it (4 to 13) and, from the third, glints.
- **The stall gets busy:** each served customer joins a small crowd at the back of the stall (0.45 of full size, behind the board and the counter, popping up as it joins, bobbing and swaying; at most four; not in tall windows). The first and second served customers each bring up a string of bunting along the awning (dropping in from 46u above with a bounce, 0.6 s, with `paper-rustle` and chips). The bunting flags are decoration, never counted.
- **Step 9's merge:** a pop and chips as the tags meet (see "Discovery without words").
- **The round end** (next section) and a celebration with linocut confetti and gulls.

All particles are one pooled set of 240 sprite particles (`src/games/market-stall/fx.ts`): carved chips (three shard shapes in each of six colours: the four inks, plus the coins' copper and silver), a four-point cream glint and a two-frame ink gull, each baked once per size on a CPU canvas.

## Round end: closing the till

When the last customer leaves, before the celebration (owner's approved ideas 1 and 2, 2026-10-05):

1. **The till opens** (0 to 0.45 s): a till tray holding what the round took (a coin of each kind, front up with its value tag, and a bill of each kind the round's customers paid with, so a dollar round shows bills; a $1 or $5 bill paying cents counts as a bill) rises at the bottom middle with an overshoot and `till-drawer`. The pieces sit in one row, coins then bills, each in its own slot as wide as the piece plus a gap (8u), so none overlaps another; when the row is wider than 0.88 of the tray, every piece shrinks by the same factor (the closing tray is not a target, so these may draw under 96 px); the slate (the board's art, baked once per window size) drops in from the top.
2. **Each sale pours** (0.55 s a sale): the sale's price tag (the price as it was sold: "26¢", "$1.25" or "$7", the total for a two-item sale) flies out of the till to a row under the slate and lands with a `pop` and chips; its amount pours into the slate's cups, one cup per dollar in the left grid (under a "$") and one per cent in the right grid (under a "¢"), each cup popping in with a climbing `tick`. A grid shows only when the round has that kind of sale (the dollar grid also when the cents reach 100). A dollar cup is deep ink with a mustard ring and nearly fills its pitch (0.98 of it), where a cent cup is mustard with an ink ring at 0.9 of the pitch, so the two never look alike. When the cents reach 100, those 100 cups trade for one dollar cup with a `pop-big`, glints and chips, and the cents start again from 0. A running total sits at the top of the slate at 0.82 of its size.
3. **The total** (0.1 s after the last sale): the total pops to full size with a `pop-big` and chips: "$14.30" when there are dollars and cents, "$14" or "30¢" otherwise (the running total above the cups is written the same way). It is exactly the sum of the round's sale prices; the debug stats' `closing` lists every sale and the total in whole dollars and the cents that do not make a dollar. Hold 0.8 s.
4. **The takings buy something for the harbour** (1.85 s): a stream of silver, copper and cream chips (the coins' metals and the bills' paper) flies from the total at the top of the slate (from the end of it nearest the harbour spot, so it never crosses the cups) to a spot in the harbour (`whoosh` C), the slate, tags and till leave, and the change appears there with `harbour-chime`, chips while it is revealed, then three gulls lift off and glints. One change a round, in turn (round count): new sails on the two boats (wiped in from the bottom up, the small boat's a little later), the lighthouse lamp switching on (up, one soft dip, then full: one change in a second over a small area), or fresh paint on the houses (wiped in from the left). The overlays never grow: they draw at the background's own scale (1.0 at 1920x1080) and appear by wipes and fades only.
5. Then the celebration.

The child sees what their work earned and that it helps the town. Nothing is bought for the child; there is no balance, nothing is kept (the change, the bunting and the crowd are gone when the next round starts, and none of it is saved), and the takings never touch stars or stickers (they are awarded the same whatever the total). A press or key during the count (after its first 0.5 s) plays the rest four times faster (about 1.3 s instead of 5 s for four sales); Escape and the corner Home button leave as at any time (the round's awards were already saved).

## Mistake handling

There is no wrong state. A coin or bill that is too much hops back to its well with a soft giggle (three quiet `pop` A notes) and the customer smiles; no error sound, no red, no shake. A piece dropped on empty space slides back to its well with a `whoosh`. Coins and bills can never be lost: any piece not given as change always returns to the till. Repeated presses while a piece is still flying or pouring send nothing more; the pressed piece gives a small hop.

## Keyboard-only and mouse-only play

- **Mouse or trackpad only:** every action is a press, a drag or click to carry (click, move, click); any mouse button counts for each click. A full round can be played by click to carry alone. The context menu is cancelled.
- **The big cursor** (`src/engine/cursor.ts`, `docs/design.md` "Cursor"): the scene's `hoverAt` uses the same hit tests and gates as a press. An open hand over a well or slot whose piece a press would pick up (change counting, not during the goal's three pennies, a demonstration, a piece flying to the paw, or the input guard); a closed hand anywhere while a piece is carried; a pointing hand over the corner Home, the sticker offers and Again and Home at rest, anywhere once the celebration can be skipped, and anywhere during the till count after its first 0.5 s until a press has sped it up; the arrow elsewhere, including presses that only make the customer or a well hop. The sound button reports itself. Hover cues, eased in and out over about 0.1 s: a soft cream rounded outline round the hovered well with its top piece lifted 4u and swollen to 1.06 (by the draw's scale, never a new canvas), the same outline round the paw zone while a carried piece is over it, and a 1.08 swell with a cream ring on the corner Home. Offers and Again and Home already show the focus ring when the mouse is over them.
- **Keyboard only:** a bobbing arrow and a warm ring mark the highlighted target.
  - The highlight starts on the well or slot of the next piece counting up (the same well the hint uses). Arrows move through every well or slot of the till, in reading order, including kinds that are too much. Until the child presses an arrow for this customer, the highlight follows counting up after every piece (pennies to the next 5, a nickel to the next 10, then dimes, and in dollars and cents a $1 bill from each whole dollar; $1, $5 and $10 bills the same way), so a child who only presses one key counts up the way the demonstration showed; once the child moves it with an arrow it stays where the child put it. Any other key sends the highlighted piece to the paw (at most one every 150 ms, and none while a piece is flying or pouring: the highlighted piece hops instead). A piece that is too much hops back, and the highlight then moves to the next counting-up piece that fits, so a child who only presses one key still finishes every customer.
  - There is only one drop target (the paw), so keys never need to aim a carried piece. A piece the mouse picked up and a key then sends counts as a key action: never a motor attempt, and the customer records evidence only under the arrow-and-0.4-s rule.
  - During play every key plays, Escape, Tab and Enter included. After the round, Escape goes home and Tab reaches the corner Home and sound buttons; choice and rest follow the shared rule (first key shows focus, arrows move, the next key chooses).

## How code draws amounts, cups and numerals

- Numerals, $, ¢ and the decimal point come from the bundled Andika font (`src/app/font.ts`, loaded before any bake), baked once per size into glyph strips on CPU canvases: "0123456789$¢." in deep ink (`#1d3461`) for tags, the board's counter and payment line and the paw's card, and in orange-red for the counter while change is counting. A numeral is drawn by copying glyphs from its strip at whole pixels. The $ is drawn at 0.72 of the digit size and raised 0.12 of the em, before the number ($13); the ¢ follows the number at full size (26¢). An amount in cents from 100 up is written as dollars with a decimal point and two digits ("$1.25", "$2.00").
- **Bill numerals:** every bill shows "$1", "$5", "$10" or "$20" in the plain area left of the animal (0.06 to 0.46 of the bill's width), centred at 0.27 of its width and 0.5 of its height, digits 0.34 of the bill's height (41 px on a 240 x 120 bill, 37 px at tier 1, 33 px at the 96 px floor), so "$20" fits the plain area and every denomination's digits are the same size. The same numeral is drawn on bills in the dish, in the till, in flight and in the paw, all at the full bill size.
- **Tags** show the price ("26¢", "$1.25" or "$7"); at step 9 the merged tag shows the total. **The board** always shows, as numbers, the price, the payment and the change given so far: the counter shows the price until change counting starts, then the price plus the change given, climbing to the payment; the payment line shows the dish's sign and the payment, climbing as it pours. At steps 1 to 3 the cups show the same amounts as dots (the price yellow, the change owed orange-red, turning cream as it is given). From step 4 nothing on the board shows the change owed, as dots or as a number: the child works it out from the price and the payment (owner, round RD1: the board stopped giving the answer away). The debug stats' `board` reports what the board shows.
- Cups are baked once per size (unlit, yellow, orange-red, cream) on CPU canvases; the dots a bouncing piece shows use one baked dot.
- **Coin value tags:** see "Money labels".
- Bills are baked once per size with their numeral (sprite and numeral on one CPU canvas), so a bill draws as one image anywhere.
- Nothing counted or exact is in any image: every numeral, $, ¢, cup and dot is drawn in code, and the bills' numerals are drawn in code on blank art.

## Money labels

Owner, 2026-10-06: "it needs to be SUPER clear what each coin is and how much its worth"; "play money bills are fine, they just need to be clearly labeled as $1, $5, etc. They don't need to be 'photo realistic' they can be stylized as long as its clear how much its worth"; "decimal points are fine". The rules (shared with Coin Vault):

1. **One face per coin.** Every coin in play shows its front (penny Lincoln, nickel Jefferson, dime Roosevelt, quarter Washington): in the till's wells and the rims of the stack below, the dish, the paw, while carried, in flight, in demonstrations and hints, and in the closing till tray. The back shows only for the instant a coin turns over in its flight to the paw. Nothing chooses a face at random.
2. **A value tag on every coin.** A cream rounded pill with a deep-ink edge carrying "1¢", "5¢", "10¢" or "25¢" in deep ink, in the bundled font, baked once per coin size on a CPU canvas (never per frame). It is centred on the coin's lower edge (half on the coin, half below it) and is part of the piece: it moves, lifts, scales, swells on hover, jiggles and turns with the coin. Its font is 0.46 of the coin's diameter and the pill 0.96 of the font tall (0.44 of the diameter), so the digits' ink stands 0.36 of the diameter (measured in Chrome with the bundled font): on the smallest coin drawn at 1366x768 (the 96 px dime of tier 2 and of the dish) the digits are 35 px tall, over the 20 px floor. The tag covers only the coin's lowest fifth, below the portrait's face. Every coin's tag looks the same. Tags draw after every coin of the layer (dish, till, paw, flights, the carried piece and the hand's piece; the closing till tray), so no coin ever covers part of a tag and makes it read another value. A stack shows only its top coin's tag: a well's rims below have none, the paw's fan shows its newest coin's tag and the settled column its highest coin's (the card above the column gives the column's value). In the dish coins sit one slot apart so every tag shows.
3. **Bills read "$1", "$5", "$10", "$20"**: the big code-drawn numeral on every bill has its $ sign (see "How code draws amounts"). Bills stay stylized play money; only the label is drawn.
4. **Every place that holds a kind says what it holds.** Each till well and bill slot always shows its kind's front with its value tag (the endless stack's top piece); while that piece is carried (by the child, or by the demonstrating hand), the well reads empty, as in Coin Count Vault and Ride Fare: a dashed ring (cream, so it shows in the dark well) with the coin's front at 0.18 strength and its tag at 0.35, so it still says "10¢" and never reads as a second real coin; a bill slot shows a dashed deep-ink outline with the bill at 0.3 (its numeral is part of the bill's bake). The ring and outline are baked once per size on CPU canvases. The wooden dish holds the payment, whose coins carry their own tags.
5. **Sizes stay true:** the tag never changes a coin's size (the dime stays smaller than the nickel at the true ratio, at least 96 px at 1366x768). Hit areas are unchanged.

## Performance

No allocation in update or render: flights (payment drops, change, bounces and returns, 24) and particles (240) are pooled; the dish's pieces (16) and the paw's pieces (100) are fixed arrays. Sprites draw from the sprite store's scaled canvases, one per sprite and size: anything that grows or shrinks while it moves (a good gliding into the customer's arms, a tag moving with it, a dish coin drawn at the smaller dish size, the board's dish sign) draws its one cached canvas at its largest size and is scaled at draw time, so no animation makes new canvases (round 5 found a gliding item and the old purse making one per frame); which are released when the window size, pixel ratio or tier changes, and on exit. Cups, the dot, the glow, the glyph strips, the four coin value tags, the bills with their numerals, the board frame and the till tray are baked on CPU canvases (`willReadFrequently: true`, pitfalls 2026-10-03) when their size changes, never per frame. Board frames and till trays are kept by size: every board height (no cups, or 1 to 5 rows of cups) bakes ahead in idle periods once per window size, and a new customer's till tray bakes in an idle period before the till slides up (only if none came, on the frame the till first shows). The background is rescaled only when the canvas size or pixel ratio changes; at 765 KB it is decoded ahead by `src/engine/sprites.ts`'s idle decode. No gradients, `shadowBlur` or `fillText` per frame (the glow and cup gradients, the coin tags' text and the bill numerals' text are drawn once, in bakes). The customer is clipped with one rectangle `clip()` per frame. Art is scaled ahead in idle periods during play (up to four sprites per idle period while 6 ms remain, each then drawn once nearly invisible), at exactly the sizes play draws them: first what play needs (both faces of every coin, the wells for every largest coin, the bill slot, every customer's two poses, the helper hand, the board's dish sign, every good at its front and step-9 sizes), then the celebration's and rest's happy poses and goods. ## Currency illustrations

The legal finding is in `docs/research/08-money-grades-1-2.md` section 5 (not legal advice; a reading of public statutes, regulations and agency pages):

- 18 U.S.C. 504 permits illustrations of US currency only in black and white and at under 75 percent or over 150 percent of the real note's size in each dimension, with the plates destroyed after final use; 31 CFR 411.1 allows colour illustrations under the same size rule if they are one-sided and every file used to make them is destroyed after final use. 18 U.S.C. 474 makes an impression "in the likeness of" a note, "or any part thereof", a crime outside those exceptions. A published web game cannot meet the destruction condition (its image files stay on the server and in every browser cache), so it cannot rely on the illustration exceptions at all.
- An original, stylized toy bill with no element of a real note is not an illustration or likeness of any genuine note, in 08's reading. Inside the limits below, 08 found no meaningful risk, so the owner's stop condition was not triggered.

Design limits this game follows (08 section 5, points 1 to 9, plus the owner's 2026-10-05 note that each bill must clearly read as 1, 5, 10 or 20):

1. Original art only: the bill sprites were generated from text prompts with the game's concept image (and, for $5, $10 and $20, the game's own processed $1 bill) as the only image inputs. No real note, scan or photo of one was given to the image model.
2. No real-note elements: no portrait of a person (an animal silhouette instead: gull $1, fish $5, crab $10, whale $20), no seals, serial numbers, signatures, lettering, lathe-work or guilloche borders, security-thread or watermark look, buildings, corner circles or inner frame.
3. Not a real note's colours: every bill is the same butter-cream paper with a deep-blue outline and an orange-red animal, one toy palette; colour never stands for value.
4. Non-genuine proportions: exactly 2:1 (a real note is about 2.35:1), with big rounded corners and a thick soft outline.
5. Size: every drawn bill is at most 240 x 120 CSS px at 1366x768 (cap 280 x 140), about 61 x 30 mm on the children's 15.6-inch laptop, well under 75 percent of a real note (156 x 66.3 mm); short side never under 96 CSS px.
6. One side only: bills never flip and have no back.
7. Visibly a toy: flat linocut colour, a big code-drawn numeral with $, a friendly animal.
8. No bill-shaped advertising (18 U.S.C. 475).
9. Recorded here and in the asset table (each bill marked original art).

Coins look like real US coins, the same set Piggy Parade uses (round RC1, 2026-10-06). The owner asked for it: "the coins need to be significantly more USA-actual change looking. currently you can not tell what a dime vs nickel is for example". The earlier linocut coins (invented children's faces, with the nickel, dime and quarter all deep blue on cream) are gone. Pictures of coins are allowed: 18 U.S.C. 489 covers physical tokens in the likeness of a coin, and a 1951 amendment removed the language that could restrict coin pictures in publications (08 section 5). Each coin shows its real motif in soft metal relief: a copper penny with Lincoln and the shield, a smooth-edged silver nickel with Jefferson and Monticello, a smaller reeded silver dime with Roosevelt and the torch between branches, and a reeded silver quarter with Washington and the eagle. No inscriptions, lettering, numbers or dates on any face.

**Decimal point (owner decision, 2026-10-06: "decimal points are fine").** Amounts counted in cents are written in ¢ under $1 ("75¢") and with a decimal point from $1 up ("$1.25", "$2.00", the counter passing $1.00); whole-dollar amounts at the $ steps stay "$7"; the closing total is "$14.30". Before round RD1 no amount used a decimal point (Common Core, Florida, New York and North Carolina leave it out of grade 2; Texas and Virginia teach "$0.45"), and the dollars-and-cents step 8 needs it.

## Art

Medium: hand-carved linocut block print in four inks (deep blue, warm orange-red, mustard yellow, warm cream paper), bold carved gouge marks, slightly imperfect ink coverage and paper grain, flat shapes, matching the concept. The coins are the exception: they are Piggy Parade's metal coins, so a child sees one set of US coins across the hub (see "Currency illustrations"). Shared controls (Home, sound, play arrow) reuse the existing art. Everything counted or exact is drawn in code (see above). No image contains text, letters, numerals or a group of things to count.

All bitmaps except the coins were generated on 2026-10-05 with the built-in `image_gen` tool in Codex CLI 0.159.0 (`codex exec -m gpt-6-astra -c model_reasoning_effort=medium -c model_provider=openai`). The tool did not report the image model. Every request attached the selected concept (`ms2.png`, 1672x941) as its first reference and shared one style block (the linocut description above, with no text, letters, numbers, dollar or cent signs, logos, inscriptions or interface). Second references: the earlier $1 bill for the other bills, the till tray for the bill slot, the first board for the final board, the heron customer for the heron sticker and the tile. Sprite requests asked for a transparent background; every result came back with a clean alpha channel.

Processing (`sharp`, scripts kept with the sources): regions of alpha above 128 were found and isolated, alpha under 16 cleared and over 235 made solid, colours at soft edges replaced by nearby solid colours (no dark or light fringe), specks under 300 px removed, cropped to the alpha bounds with 4 px padding, resized with Lanczos 3 and written as WebP quality 80, alpha quality 50. The two poses of each customer share one scale (waiting pose 760 px tall); both are anchored at the feet. Bills: made exactly 2:1 by resampling only the plain cream run between the left corners and the animal (uniform along the width, so the outline, corners and animal keep their shape), then 512x256. The bill slot was made 1.85:1 the same way along its height, between its top and bottom rims. Each file was checked on mid-grey and dark grounds (contact sheet with the sources) and at 1:1 for halos.

The eight coin faces are byte-for-byte copies of `public/art/piggy-parade/coin-*-{heads,tails}.webp` (320x320, made for Piggy Parade on 2026-10-04 with the same Codex `image_gen` tool; prompts, raw images and processing are recorded in `docs/games/piggy-parade.md` under "Art": square box around the disc's centre, exact round alpha edge, WebP quality 80, alpha quality 50). They replaced this game's 384x384 linocut coins in round RC1. The largest coin this game draws at 1920x1080 is 291 px wide (the tier-0 quarter, 228 px, in the 1.28 pick-up squash), under the files' 320 px, so no new larger set was needed.

`harbour-stall.webp` follows the three-strip method (PR #13, as Ride Fare): the 1536x1024 base was cut into three overlapping 683x1024 portrait strips, each enlarged to 1024x1536 (Lanczos 3) and redrawn in its own request with the concept attached; the stitch script registered each strip to the base enlarged 1.5 times (alignment error 11.8 to 14.2), replaced each strip's broad colour (Gaussian sigma 32 px) with the base's, joined neighbours along the vertical path of least difference (6 px feather), and resized the 2304x1536 mosaic to 1920x1280 (Lanczos 3); WebP quality 80, opaque. No visible join at 1:1. It shows the striped awning edge, the two stall posts, the harbour (sky, gulls, sea, two boats, pilings, a jetty, the lighthouse and houses) and the empty plank counter with a small loaf basket and a wheat jug at its far ends: no customer, coins, bills, dish, till, tag, board or goods.
