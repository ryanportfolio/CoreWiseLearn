# Market Stall

A round game about paying and getting change. The child runs a bread stall on a harbour, seen from behind the counter. Animal customers come to the counter one at a time with something to buy: a loaf, a pretzel, a pie. Each item has a price tag, and the customer puts a payment in the wooden dish on the counter. The child checks whether the payment is enough and, when it is more than the price, counts the change back from the till into the customer's open paw, counting up from the price (27, 28, 29, 30). Then the customer takes the item and the change and leaves happily. It covers Common Core 2.MD.C.8 (money word problems with $ and ¢). Learning tag: `counting` (the shared list of learning tags has no `money`; adding one is a change to shared code and needs the owner's OK).

Nothing is bought or kept by the child. The child is the seller and serves someone else; the till refills every round; there is no wallet, balance, shop or goal that spans sessions, and money never touches stars or stickers (`docs/research/07-money.md` section 2; `docs/research/08-money-grades-1-2.md` section 6, "Shared rules"). The selected concept is `docs/games/market-stall/concept.webp` (the owner's pick, MS2, 2026-10-05).

Market Stall starts where Ride Fare stops (fares to 99¢ with pennies, nickels and dimes, and handing back 1 to 9 pennies from a dime) and adds quarters, bills, the $ sign, counting up the change and change from $1 and $20.

## The action and its response

There are two kinds of action. Which one a customer asks for depends on the learning step (table below).

- **Decide: enough, or more please.** The item with its price tag and the customer's closed purse sit on the counter. Pressing the item hands it over ("enough"); pressing the purse asks for more. Both are single presses with any mouse button or a tap. The item lifts and glides into the customer's arms (0.4 s) or the purse gives a small hop (0.2 s).
- **Count out the change.** The till holds coins in round wells (or bills in long slots). Carry a coin to the customer's open paw in either of two ways, at every motor tier and with any mouse button: drag it there with the button held and let go, or click it (press and release without moving 24 px) and it follows the pointer with no button held until the next click, which puts it down. Put down on the paw zone (or within the tier's snap distance of it) it pays; put down anywhere else it hops gently back to its well. While carried the piece is drawn lifted (1.12 of its size, 12u higher, over a baked soft shadow), the top of its well's stack is gone, and the paw's warm glow grows. Bills work the same way. (Owner, 2026-10-05: click to carry everywhere in the hub; it replaces round 2's click-then-click and tier 0's one-press send, so there is one behaviour.)
- **Each piece of change pays at once:** the coin flies to the paw (0.3 s) and joins a compact stack there at full size, drawn over the till: the newest piece in the paw zone (its lower edge never below the zone's), each of the four newest 0.12 of a coin up and to the left of the one after it (bills step half as far sideways), so every piece shows an edge and none draws under its size floor. From the fifth piece on, the older pieces settle in a column just left of those four, oldest at the bottom, each 0.1 of a piece above the one before (closing up so the column rises at most 0.45 of a piece), under a cream card with an ink edge showing their value ("91¢", "$6") in the tags' numerals. The paw keeps every piece (up to 100: a 5¢ item paid with $1 can take 95 pennies), so the four pieces in the fan and the card always add up to the change given; round 5 fixed a paw that stopped drawing pieces after 24. A wide bill whose stack would reach the till is lifted above it. The counter numeral on the board climbs by the coin's value, one step per cent with a soft rising tick (a penny one step; a nickel five steps 60 ms apart; a dime ten steps 30 ms apart; a quarter 25 steps 14 ms apart, so every pour takes about 0.3 s), and the change cups on the board turn from orange-red to cream as the counter passes them. A $1 bill adds one dollar, a $5 bill five, a $10 bill ten, with the same tick pour.
- **Too much:** a coin or bill worth more than the change still owed touches the paw, jiggles there for 0.9 s with its value shown just above it as solid deep-ink dots on a cream card with an ink edge (dots about 0.13 of a penny across, at least 12 px; rows of ten split 5 and 5, so a quarter shows two rows of ten and a five, a $20 bill two rows of ten), and hops back to its well (0.5 s) with a soft giggle while the customer smiles. Nothing changes on the board.
- **Done:** when the counter reaches the payment, the board's cups pulse once (0.35 s), the customer switches to its happy pose, the item glides into its arms (0.4 s), and it walks off to the right with the change and the item (0.8 s). The next customer walks in from the left (0.6 s), sets the item down, the tag swings, and the payment drops from its paw into the dish (coins 0.35 s each, one after another 0.12 s apart; a bill flutters down in 0.5 s, turning a little but always at its full size, and no dish piece is ever a target). The payment then pours into the board's cups, largest piece first, each piece hopping as its cups light (about 0.3 s a piece, at most 0.07 s a cup; a $1 bill paying 100¢ pours its 100 cups in 1.2 s with one tick a row). At change steps the till slides up from the bottom edge during the drop and pour (0.35 s); at step 5 it slides up once the decision starts the change. The child can act as soon as the pour ends.
- **The dish** shows the payment at full size (every coin and bill at its tier size, never smaller). Pieces of one kind fan from left to right, each one covering the right part of the one before (coins 0.42 of a diameter apart, closing to 0.3 when room is short; bills 0.38 of a width apart and never closer, since a bill's numeral ends at 0.37 of its width), so each coin's edge and each bill's numeral stays visible; kinds follow largest first, in at most two rows (three only when two cannot fit the widest dish). A lower row of bills sits 0.74 of a bill below the row above, under that bill's numeral (round 4: a $5 used to cover the bottom of a $10's numeral). A top-up's pieces join after the payment's. The dish never grows taller for its pile and never reaches above the counter's back edge, so it never covers the customer or a price tag; a pile that needs more height reaches past the dish's rim (that footprint counts as the dish's in the layout).
- **The customers** are content, curious or delighted. None is ever shown waiting impatiently, sad, pleading or disappointed.

### Enough or more: what happens after each choice

Rules from 08 section 6 notes; no choice is ever wrong on screen.

| Payment | Child hands the item over | Child asks for more (purse) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact | The customer takes the item and leaves happily. Counted right. | The purse opens, tips upside down and shakes, a little larger (0.9 s): it is empty. The customer laughs, takes the item and leaves happily. Counted not right. |
| Short | The customer looks at the board, smiles, and adds the coin or bill that makes it exact from its purse; the unlit cups fill; the customer takes the item and leaves. Counted not right. | The customer nods, takes coins or bills from the purse into the dish until the payment is exact; the unlit cups fill; the item then glows and the child hands it over (this second press records nothing). Counted right. |
| More (step 5 only) | Change counting starts: the till slides up, the item and its tag glide to the front spot in front of the customer and the purse fades away. Counted right for the decision. | The purse opens, tips over and shows it is empty; then change counting starts the same way. Counted not right for the decision. |

A step-5 customer who is owed change records one result when the change is done: right only when the decision was right and the change was exact with no piece bounced.

## Discovery without words

The very first round shows the goal first: the otter walks in (0.6 s), the board shows a price of 27¢ with 30 cups (27 yellow, 3 orange-red change cups) and three dimes lie in the dish, three pennies hop from the till into its paw 0.5 s apart while the counter climbs 28, 29, 30, and the otter waves and walks off with its loaf (about 3.3 s in all; presses meanwhile make the otter hop). Then the heron steps up with a 26¢ pretzel and three dimes; once the dimes have poured and the till has slid up, the linocut helper hand rises from the bottom edge, presses a penny in the till, carries it to the heron's paw and lets go; the counter climbs to 27 (the hand carries the penny about 6.5 to 7.5 s after the game opens, measured in round 2). The hand then rests on the well of the next helpful piece (the penny well here) and taps it in a loop until the child does anything; the keyboard highlight moves to the same well, so the two cues agree. The child finishes the change with three pennies. The introduction's other customers come from the child's current step. The introduction records no evidence, changes no motor tier and always earns three stars.

Idle: after 6 quiet seconds the hand shows the next helpful action, then fades, and repeats every 7 s while the child stays idle. In change counting it carries a see-through hint coin (in a pulsing warm halo, paying nothing) from the well of the next piece when counting up from the price to the paw, and the keyboard highlight moves to that well. Counting up, as every demonstration teaches: ones (pennies or $1 bills) to the next 5, a five (a nickel or $5 bill) to the next 10, then tens (dimes or $10 bills); when that kind is not in the till or is too much, the largest smaller kind that fits, else the largest that fits. In a decision it taps the board's unlit cups (short) or the full rows (exact), then rests over the purse (short) or the item (exact). A customer finished after a hint still leaves happily but records no learning evidence. After two bounces on one customer, the hint comes after 4 s instead of 6 s (a customer has only one decision) (08 section 6, "Easing after misses").

**First-time demonstrations.** The first customer whose content is a new step for a profile gets one ghost-hand demonstration of that step's new idea; the save remembers which demonstrations the profile has seen (`demos`: bit n for step n's idea, and bit 0 for step 1's short payment, since step 1 has two), so each plays once per profile. The introduction's taught customer counts as step 2's demonstration. A warm-up customer gets its content's demonstration too. The demonstrated customer records no evidence.

- Step 1: the first exact payment: the hand presses the item (it goes to the customer). The first short payment: the hand taps the unlit cups, then the purse; the customer adds a coin.
- Step 2: as in the introduction (one penny to the paw, the child finishes).
- Step 3: the first bill customer: its bills drop into the dish and pour one cup per dollar; the hand taps the price tag, then the lit cups, and fades; the child then decides.
- Step 4: the hand carries one $1 bill from the till to the paw; the counter climbs from the price by 1.
- Step 5: once the payment (which always holds a quarter) has poured, the hand points at the quarter in the dish, then at the lit cups (the quarter's 25 are two full rows of ten and five more), and fades; the child then decides.
- Step 6: the first step-6 customer is always 23¢ paid with two quarters: the hand moves a penny (24), a penny (25), then a nickel (30), and taps the dime well; the child finishes with two dimes (40, 50).
- Step 7: the $1 bill drops into the dish and 100 cups pour, ten rows of ten; the hand moves the first coin of counting up (a penny to the next 5, a nickel to the next 10, else a dime).
- Step 8: the first step-8 customer is always $13 paid with $20: the hand moves a $1 bill (14), another (15), and taps the $5 slot; the child gives the $5 (20).
- Step 9: first the two tags slide together into one tag showing the total, and the board shows the total the moment they meet (with a small pop and chips); then the second item's cups ride back along their rows into place after the first item's, all together like a belt, so neighbours stay at least one pitch apart (a cup passing the start of a row shrinks out there and grows in at the end of the row above; no cup ever overlaps another). Then the payment drops (1.3 s in all, the tags taking the first 0.45 of it; the first time 2.1 s, with the hand following the second tag into the first).
- After every carry demonstration the hand taps the well of the next helpful piece, and the keyboard highlight moves there too.

## Round structure and attempts

1. **Customers:** every round counts at least three customers (round 4, so the clean-round rule can always fire): at step 1, 3 at motor tier 0 and 4 at tiers 1 and 2; above step 1 the warm-up customer comes on top, so 4 at every tier (the introduction: the goal, then 3). Six customers (heron, otter, pelican, puffin, seal, bear) take turns in a fixed rotation that continues across rounds (`turn` in the save); each brings one of eight goods (round loaf, long loaf, braided loaf, pretzel, fish biscuit, pie, honey jar, cake), never the same good twice in a row. Above step 1 the first customer of a round comes from the step below (a warm-up that records nothing); the rest come from the current step. At step 9, the top, the warm-up comes from step 7 and step 8 in turn, round by round, and the other three customers are step-9 customers (each in cents or in dollars at random), so every round counts three and still varies.
2. **Closing the till** (round 4; see "Round end" below): the day's takings are counted and buy something for the harbour.
3. **Celebration:** the round's customers stand in a row behind the counter (clipped at its back edge, half the window tall at most), each in its happy pose with its good in front, bobbing and waving, the row kept within the middle 56 percent of the window so the harbour's change stays in view; linocut confetti (carved chips in the four inks) bursts from the middle and two flocks of gulls lift off; the lighthouse lamp, when it was this round's change, glows gently; stars land one by one. Input is ignored for the first 1.5 s and until the last star lands (1.56 s for three stars); it ends by itself at 4.6 s.
4. **Sticker choice:** while this game's six stickers are not all owned and `rewardsEnabled` is true, the shared `createStickerOffers` helper (`src/ui/sticker-offer.ts`, reused, not copied) shows two stickers standing on the counter, with the small sticker book beside them; its `warm` and `warmBook` run during the celebration. Same guard as every game: input ignored for 1.2 s, nothing focused, the first key only shows focus.
5. **Still rest:** the empty stall with the harbour (its bunting and this round's change still up, all still), the sticker book with the new sticker (or the round's customers when there is no gift, standing still: no bobbing or swaying at rest), the stars, and Again and Home of equal size and colour (mustard circles, 62u radius, at least 48 px). `services.roundBoundary()` runs when rest is reached.

Stars: every finished round earns 3. Bounces and not-right choices never cost a star. Awards persist once, as in Ride Fare and Bubble Bay: round count, stars and the offered pair go into the game bag's `pending` field with a unique round `id` and `save.flush()` when the last customer leaves, before the till count and the celebration show; leaving or reloading returns to the choice or the rest; leaving rest by any route clears `pending`. The till, the dish, every payment, every coin and bill, the till count, the harbour change, the bunting and the crowd are play material only and are never saved; the next round starts with none of them.

**Attempts (motor):** one carry (by drag or by click to carry) or one decision press counts once, whatever the input style. Releasing a carried coin or bill on the paw zone (with the tier's snap distance) is one hit; releasing it anywhere off the till is one miss and it slides back to its well (released over the till, it just goes back). A press on the item or the purse during a decision is one hit; a press on empty play space is one miss. A coin that bounces back because it was too much still reached the target and is a hit. Picking up, key presses, demonstrations and hints never count. At the end of a round: 8 or more attempts with under 70 percent hits moves down a tier; 12 or more at 90 percent or better is a qualifying round, and two in a row move up a tier (Ride Fare's rule).

A customer whose deciding press or any change piece was not deliberate records nothing, so a stream of quick presses plays fully but counts nothing.

Steps 1 and 2 need nothing Ride Fare did not teach except the yes-or-more choice and counting up past 10, so a strong 6-year-old can reach them. Step 3 of 08 section 6 (bills, the $ sign and dollar change together) is split here into steps 3 and 4 so each step adds one idea (R1 audit). Steps 5 to 9 follow 08 steps 4 to 8. Amounts never mix $ and ¢ in one tag; a payment is all coins or all bills, except the $1 bill at steps 7 and 9, which pours its 100¢ as cups before the change. No decimal point anywhere (see "Currency illustrations").

**Built:** all nine steps (steps 1 to 4 in round 2, steps 5 to 9 in round 3); step 9 is the top step, and round 2's temporary cap at step 5 is gone. Step 4 pays with a $5 bill (prices $1 to $4, half of them) or a $10 bill, so its change is 1 to 9 dollars.

**Moving between steps** (between rounds only; 08 section 6, copied from Ride Fare): at the current step, move up one step when every counted customer of the round was right and there were at least 3, or when 6 of the step's last 8 counted customers were right; move down one step when 2 or fewer of the last 6 were right (never below step 1). A step change resets that step's window. Every round above step 1 opens with one warm-up customer from the step below.

**Easing and no stall.** Every task can always be finished: the till's wells never run out (each well shows a stack of four coins with offset rims and refills from below), a coin or bill that is too much just bounces, and the change owed is always reachable with the till's kinds (pennies or $1 bills are always present when change is owed, at every tier). A short payment always becomes exact (the customer tops it up after either choice). A child who struggles at a step moves back one step and gets full rounds of that content. A keyboard-only child who never uses the arrows records no evidence; after 3 finished rounds at step 1 or 2 with no counted customer, the step moves up by one, up to step 3; beyond that only evidence moves it. Struggling inside a round only brings the idle hint sooner; the content never changes mid-round.

## Feel and feedback

Owner, 2026-10-05: "what keeps people engaged is the visuals, the sounds, the feedback, so lets go all out on that aspect", and "make sure its fun and enjoyable to learn about money and wanting to earn it". Round 4 added this layer. Every effect is warm and calm: nothing flashes, nothing is shrill, no screen shake, nothing waits for an effect to finish (every effect runs alongside play), and none is a reward for being right (a bounced piece lands with the same feel).

- **Pick-up:** the piece squashes wide (1.28 by 0.99 of its size) and springs back to its lifted 1.12 over 0.35 s, rising 12u above the pointer over a baked soft ink shadow; it sways a little with the pointer's speed. Two cream glints at its well; coins play a quiet `pop` B, bills `paper-rustle`.
- **Flight to the paw:** an arc (0.3 s); a coin turns over once (its face flips to tails and back as it turns), a bill flutters.
- **Landing:** the newest piece in the paw squashes and settles (0.3 s); a `coin-stack` clink whose pitch climbs with the stack (bills: `paper-rustle`); six carved chips in the four inks and a glint.
- **Payment into the dish:** each piece lands with a hop, three small chips and a `coin-stack` clink climbing piece by piece (bills: `paper-rustle`).
- **Cups:** each cup pops in coming down from 1.35 of its size, dipping just under and settling (0.3 s), with the rising `tick` as before.
- **The till** rises with a small overshoot, like a drawer popping open, with `till-drawer`.
- **Happy customers, more each time:** when a customer is paid (or takes its item) it cheers, and the cheer grows with each customer of the round: the first hops once; the second hops twice with a wave (a sway); the third hops three times with a squash on each landing; the fourth dances four hops. Carved chips burst from it (4 to 13) and, from the third, glints.
- **The stall gets busy:** each served customer joins a small crowd at the back of the stall (0.45 of full size, behind the board and the counter, popping up as it joins, bobbing and swaying; at most four; not in tall windows). The first and second served customers each bring up a string of bunting along the awning (dropping in from 46u above with a bounce, 0.6 s, with `paper-rustle` and chips). The bunting flags are decoration, never counted.
- **Step 9's merge:** a pop and chips as the tags meet (see "Discovery without words").
- **The round end** (next section) and a celebration with linocut confetti and gulls.

## Round end: closing the till

When the last customer leaves, before the celebration (owner's approved ideas 1 and 2, 2026-10-05):

1. **The till opens** (0 to 0.45 s): a till tray holding what the round took (a coin of each kind and a bill of each kind the round's customers paid with, so a dollar round shows bills; the $1 bill paying 100¢ counts as a bill) rises at the bottom middle with an overshoot and `till-drawer`. The pieces sit in one row, coins then bills, each in its own slot as wide as the piece plus a gap (8u), so none overlaps another; when the row is wider than 0.88 of the tray, every piece shrinks by the same factor (the closing tray is not a target, so these may draw under 96 px); the slate (the board's art, baked once per window size) drops in from the top.
2. **Each sale pours** (0.55 s a sale): the sale's price tag (the price as it was sold: "26¢" or "$7", the total for a two-item sale) flies out of the till to a row under the slate and lands with a `pop` and chips; its amount pours into the slate's cups, one cup per dollar in the left grid (under a "$") and one per cent in the right grid (under a "¢"), each cup popping in with a climbing `tick`. A grid shows only when the round has that kind of sale (the dollar grid also when the cents reach 100). A dollar cup is deep ink with a mustard ring and nearly fills its pitch (0.98 of it), where a cent cup is mustard with an ink ring at 0.9 of the pitch, so the two never look alike. When the cents reach 100, those 100 cups trade for one dollar cup with a `pop-big`, glints and chips, and the cents start again from 0. A running total sits at the top of the slate at 0.82 of its size.
3. **The total** (0.1 s after the last sale): the total pops to full size with a `pop-big` and chips: "$14 and 30¢" when there are dollars and cents, "$14" or "30¢" otherwise. It is exactly the sum of the round's sale prices, in whole dollars and the cents that do not make a dollar (no decimal point); the debug stats' `closing` lists every sale and the total. Hold 0.8 s.
4. **The takings buy something for the harbour** (1.85 s): a stream of mustard and cream chips flies from the total at the top of the slate (from the end of it nearest the harbour spot, so it never crosses the cups) to a spot in the harbour (`whoosh` C), the slate, tags and till leave, and the change appears there with `harbour-chime`, chips while it is revealed, then three gulls lift off and glints. One change a round, in turn (round count): new sails on the two boats (wiped in from the bottom up, the small boat's a little later), the lighthouse lamp switching on (up, one soft dip, then full: one change in a second over a small area), or fresh paint on the houses (wiped in from the left). The overlays never grow: they draw at the background's own scale (1.0 at 1920x1080) and appear by wipes and fades only.
5. Then the celebration.

The child sees what their work earned and that it helps the town. Nothing is bought for the child; there is no balance, nothing is kept (the change, the bunting and the crowd are gone when the next round starts, and none of it is saved), and the takings never touch stars or stickers (they are awarded the same whatever the total). A press or key during the count (after its first 0.5 s) plays the rest four times faster (about 1.3 s instead of 5 s for four sales); Escape and the corner Home button leave as at any time (the round's awards were already saved).

## Mistake handling

There is no wrong state. A coin or bill that is too much hops back to its well with a soft giggle (three quiet `pop` A notes) and the customer smiles; no error sound, no red, no shake. A piece dropped on empty space slides back to its well with a `whoosh`. A not-right decision plays out as in the table above, with the customer laughing or nodding, never frowning. Coins and bills can never be lost: any piece not given as change always returns to the till. Repeated presses while a piece is still flying or pouring send nothing more; the pressed piece gives a small hop.

## Keyboard-only and mouse-only play

- **Mouse or trackpad only:** every action is a press, a drag or click to carry (click, move, click); any mouse button counts for each click. A full round can be played by click to carry alone. The context menu is cancelled.
- **The big cursor** (`src/engine/cursor.ts`, `docs/design.md` "Cursor"): the scene's `hoverAt` uses the same hit tests and gates as a press. An open hand over a well or slot whose piece a press would pick up (change counting, not during the goal's three pennies, a demonstration, a piece flying to the paw, or the input guard); a closed hand anywhere while a piece is carried; a pointing hand over the item and the purse at a decision (the item alone while it glows), the corner Home, the sticker offers and Again and Home at rest, anywhere once the celebration can be skipped, and anywhere during the till count after its first 0.5 s until a press has sped it up; the arrow elsewhere, including presses that only make the customer or a well hop. The sound button reports itself. Hover cues, eased in and out over about 0.1 s: a soft cream rounded outline round the hovered well with its top piece lifted 4u and swollen to 1.06 (by the draw's scale, never a new canvas), the same outline round the item or purse zone and round the paw zone while a carried piece is over it, and a 1.08 swell with a cream ring on the corner Home. Offers and Again and Home already show the focus ring when the mouse is over them.
- **Keyboard only:** a bobbing arrow and a warm ring mark the highlighted target.
  - Decision: the highlight starts on the item. Left and Right (Up and Down the same) switch between the item and the purse. Any other key presses the highlighted one (at most one action every 150 ms). When the item glows (after a short payment and "more, please"), any key but an arrow hands it over.
  - Change: the highlight starts on the well or slot of the largest piece that fits the change still owed. Arrows move through every well or slot of the till, in reading order, including kinds that are too much. Until the child presses an arrow for this customer, the highlight follows counting up after every piece (the same well the hint uses: pennies to the next 5, a nickel to the next 10, then dimes; $1, $5 and $10 bills the same way), so a child who only presses one key counts up the way the demonstration showed; once the child moves it with an arrow it stays where the child put it. Any other key sends the highlighted piece to the paw (at most one every 150 ms, and none while a piece is flying or pouring: the highlighted piece hops instead). A piece that is too much hops back, and the highlight then moves to the next counting-up piece that fits, so a child who only presses one key still finishes every customer.
  - There is only one drop target in each phase (the paw in change counting; no carrying in a decision), so keys never need to aim a carried piece. A piece the mouse picked up and a key then sends counts as a key action: never a motor attempt, and the customer records evidence only under the arrow-and-0.4-s rule.
  - During play every key plays, Escape, Tab and Enter included. After the round, Escape goes home and Tab reaches the corner Home and sound buttons; choice and rest follow the shared rule (first key shows focus, arrows move, the next key chooses).

## How code draws amounts, cups and numerals

- Numerals, $ and ¢ come from the bundled Andika font (`src/app/font.ts`, loaded before any bake), baked once per size into glyph strips on CPU canvases: "0123456789$¢" in deep ink (`#1d3461`) for tags, bills and the board's counter, and in orange-red for the counter while change is counting. A numeral is drawn by copying glyphs from its strip at whole pixels. The $ is drawn at 0.72 of the digit size and raised 0.12 of the em, before the number ($13); the ¢ follows the number at full size (26¢).
- **Bill numerals:** every bill shows "$1", "$5", "$10" or "$20" in the plain area left of the animal (0.06 to 0.46 of the bill's width), centred at 0.27 of its width and 0.5 of its height, digits 0.34 of the bill's height (41 px on a 240 x 120 bill, 37 px at tier 1, 33 px at the 96 px floor), so "$20" fits the plain area and every denomination's digits are the same size. The same numeral is drawn on bills in the dish, in the till, in flight and in the paw, all at the full bill size.
- **Tags** show the price ("26¢" or "$7"); at step 9 the merged tag shows the total. **The board** shows the counter: the price while a decision is open, then the climbing count during change counting. There is no running total of the payment in numerals: the payment is shown as cups.
- Cups are baked once per size (unlit, yellow, orange-red, cream) on CPU canvases; the dots a bouncing piece shows use one baked dot.
- Bills are baked once per size with their numeral (sprite and numeral on one CPU canvas), so a bill draws as one image anywhere.
- Nothing counted or exact is in any image: every numeral, $, ¢, cup and dot is drawn in code, and the bills' numerals are drawn in code on blank art.

## Performance

No allocation in update or render: flights (payment drops, change, bounces and returns, 24) and particles (160) are pooled; the dish's pieces (16) and the paw's pieces (100) are fixed arrays. Sprites draw from the sprite store's scaled canvases, one per sprite and size: anything that grows or shrinks while it moves (a good gliding into the customer's arms or stepping to the front spot, a tag moving with it, the purse's hop and tip) draws its one cached canvas at its largest size and is scaled at draw time, so no animation makes new canvases (round 5 found the gliding item and the purse making one per frame); which are released when the window size, pixel ratio or tier changes, and on exit. Cups, the dot, the glow, the glyph strips, the bills with their numerals, the board frame and the till tray are baked on CPU canvases (`willReadFrequently: true`, pitfalls 2026-10-03) when their size changes, never per frame. Board frames and till trays are kept by size: every board height (1 to 11 rows of cups) bakes ahead in idle periods once per window size, and a new customer's till tray bakes in an idle period before the till slides up (only if none came, on the frame the till first shows). The background is rescaled only when the canvas size or pixel ratio changes; at 765 KB it is decoded ahead by `src/engine/sprites.ts`'s idle decode. No gradients, `shadowBlur` or `fillText` per frame (the glow and cup gradients and the bill numerals' text are drawn once, in bakes). The customer is clipped with one rectangle `clip()` per frame. Art is scaled ahead in idle periods during play (up to four sprites per idle period while 6 ms remain, each then drawn once nearly invisible), at exactly the sizes play draws them: first what play needs (both faces of every coin, the wells for every largest coin, the bill slot, every customer's two poses, the helper hand, every good at its decision, front and step-9 sizes), then the celebration's and rest's happy poses and goods. The fanfare is prepared ahead the same way. ## Currency illustrations

The legal finding is in `docs/research/08-money-grades-1-2.md` section 5 (not legal advice; a reading of public statutes, regulations and agency pages):

- 18 U.S.C. 504 permits illustrations of US currency only in black and white and at under 75 percent or over 150 percent of the real note's size in each dimension, with the plates destroyed after final use; 31 CFR 411.1 allows colour illustrations under the same size rule if they are one-sided and every file used to make them is destroyed after final use. 18 U.S.C. 474 makes an impression "in the likeness of" a note, "or any part thereof", a crime outside those exceptions. A published web game cannot meet the destruction condition (its image files stay on the server and in every browser cache), so it cannot rely on the illustration exceptions at all.
- An original, stylized toy bill with no element of a real note is not an illustration or likeness of any genuine note, in 08's reading. Inside the limits below, 08 found no meaningful risk, so the owner's stop condition was not triggered.

Design limits this game follows (08 section 5, points 1 to 9, plus the owner's 2026-10-05 note that each bill must clearly read as 1, 5, 10 or 20):

1. Original art only: the bill sprites were generated from text prompts with the game's concept image (and, for $5, $10 and $20, the game's own processed $1 bill) as the only image inputs. No real note, scan or photo of one was given to the image model.
2. No real-note elements: no portrait of a person (an animal silhouette instead: gull $1, fish $5, crab $10, whale $20), no seals, serial numbers, signatures, lettering, lathe-work or guilloche borders, security-thread or watermark look, buildings, corner circles or inner frame.
3. Not a real note's colours: every bill is the same butter-cream paper with a deep-blue outline and an orange-red animal, one toy palette; colour never stands for value.
4. Non-genuine proportions: exactly 2:1 (a real note is about 2.35:1), with big rounded corners and a thick soft outline.
5. Size: every drawn bill is at most 240 x 120 CSS px at 1366x768 (cap 280 x 140), about 61 x 30 mm on the children's 15.6-inch laptop, well under 75 percent of a real note (156 x 66.3 mm); short side never under 96 CSS px.
6. One side only: bills never flip and have no back.
7. Visibly a toy: flat linocut colour, a big code-drawn numeral with $, a friendly animal.
8. No bill-shaped advertising (18 U.S.C. 475).
9. Recorded here and in the asset table (each bill marked original art).

Coins are stylized linocut toy coins with a simple head and a simple tails design that echo no real coin (round 2 regenerated them: the first set echoed real US designs). Heads are generic head-and-shoulders silhouettes: a short-haired child (penny), a child with a ponytail (nickel), a child with curly hair (dime), a child in a sun hat (quarter). Tails are a sun (penny), a sailboat (nickel), an anchor (dime) and a fish (quarter). Copper penny and silver others, smooth edges on penny and nickel and reeded edges on dime and quarter, and no inscriptions, lettering, numbers or dates (08 section 5, "Coins").

**Decimal point (open decision for the owner).** No amount in this game uses a decimal point: ¢ amounts are whole cents ("26¢"), $ amounts are whole dollars ("$7"), and the only place both meet is the $1 bill pouring 100¢ (no "$1.00" is written). This follows Common Core, Florida, New York and North Carolina; Texas and Virginia teach "$0.45" in grade 2. The owner did not object to leaving decimals out; it stays open, and if the owner wants it, it would be an extra top step in Coin Vault rather than here (08 section 6, "Open questions").

## Art

Medium: hand-carved linocut block print in four inks (deep blue, warm orange-red, mustard yellow, warm cream paper), bold carved gouge marks, slightly imperfect ink coverage and paper grain, flat shapes, matching the concept. Shared controls (Home, sound, play arrow) reuse the existing art. Everything counted or exact is drawn in code (see above). No image contains text, letters, numerals or a group of things to count.

All bitmaps were generated on 2026-10-05 (coins regenerated the same day in round 2) with the built-in `image_gen` tool in Codex CLI 0.159.0 (`codex exec -m gpt-6-astra -c model_reasoning_effort=medium -c model_provider=openai`). The tool did not report the image model. Every request attached the selected concept (`ms2.png`, 1672x941) as its first reference and shared one style block (the linocut description above, with no text, letters, numbers, dollar or cent signs, logos, inscriptions or interface). Second references: the earlier $1 bill for the other bills, the till tray for the bill slot, the first board for the final board, the heron customer for the heron sticker and the tile. Sprite requests asked for a transparent background; every result came back with a clean alpha channel.
