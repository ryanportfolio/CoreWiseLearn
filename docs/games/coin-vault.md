# Coin Vault

A round game about counting money, for ages 6 to 8 (US grades 1 and 2). Animal visitors peek over the far edge of a wooden counting desk, each holding a little cloth sack of money. The child counts it for them: each coin or bill goes into its dish on a green felt mat and pours its value into a counting board of cups. Then the child picks the total from three amount tags. Later steps add vault locks that ask for an amount to be built, a $1 bill that 100 cups trade for, paper bills, and the $ and ¢ signs. Each visitor is saving for something (a bike, a kite, a boat, a scooter, a drum or a paint set) and shows it with its price. When its money is counted it rolls into a tree-stump vault, the round door slams and its dial spins shut, and a jar fills toward the goal with the counted amount: full, the visitor gets its thing; not yet, it smiles and waves. Learning tag: `counting` (themes `money`, `counting`, `animals`, as Ride Fare).

Nothing is bought or kept. The child counts someone else's money; the vault empties every round; there is no wallet, balance, shop or goal that spans sessions, and money never touches stars or stickers (`docs/research/07-money.md` section 2, `docs/research/08-money-grades-1-2.md` section 6). The selected concept is `docs/games/coin-vault/concept.webp` (the owner's pick, cv2, 2026-10-05). The research behind the steps is [08. Money for grades 1 and 2](../research/08-money-grades-1-2.md); its sections 5 and 6 are binding.

## The action and its response

- **Count a coin or bill.** Press a coin (or bill) in the row along the bottom and carry it to the mat: drag it there and let go, or click it and then click the mat. A click (a press released within 0.3 s and 24 px) picks the piece up at every motor tier: it follows the pointer with no button held, drawn lifted, until the next press puts it down. On the mat it lands as a drop there would; anywhere else it hops gently back to its place. Any mouse button counts for both clicks. Held-button dragging works as before; keys are unchanged. (Round 4, owner 2026-10-05: this replaced the tier-0 rule where one click sent a coin straight to its dish, so every tier and every game in the hub has one click-to-carry behaviour.) Where it lands:
  - The whole mat is the one drop target. A coin dropped anywhere on it, or on another kind's dish, slides over into its own dish (quarter, dime, nickel, penny, left to right): sorting is shown, never scored, and nothing bounces. The dishes show where coins go; they are not separate targets.
  - A bill in a dollars task (steps 7 and 8) goes onto one fanned pile on the mat, each new bill to the right of the last so every bill's left numeral panel stays in view.
  - A $1 bill in a cents task (step 8's "$1 and N¢" collections) flies to the counting board and lies over its upper half: it stands for the first hundred cups.
- **Each piece pours at once:** a coin flies into its dish (0.3 s) and settles there, in rows of four; at the same moment its value pours into the counting board, one cup after another: a penny lights one cup, a nickel five (70 ms apart), a dime ten (32 ms apart), a quarter twenty-five (16 ms apart, about 0.4 s). A bill in a dollars task pours one cup per dollar the same way (a $20 lights twenty cups, 20 ms apart). Each lit cup glows warm gold with a soft tick that climbs through each row of ten. The board never shows a running numeral: the cups are the count.
- **All counted:** when the row is empty, the lit cups regroup by kind, largest first (they all look alike, so nothing moves on screen), and the groups count on from the largest: the board's $1 bill if there is one, then the bills on the pile by kind ($20, $10, $5, $1), then the quarter dish, the dime dish, the nickel dish and the penny dish. Each group pulses with its cups for 0.35 s, or, when it says its running total, until the number has been spoken plus 0.12 s, so two numbers never overlap (empty groups are skipped). The first group also waits for a coin or bill name still being said. Then three amount tags slide up into the row.
- **Pick a tag:** pressing the matching tag lifts it (1.08x), it flies to the vault door and settles over the door's rectangular plate, where the amount stays in small numerals. Then the round-end sequence of the task plays (see "The task's end: vault, jar and goal" below): the money rolls into the vault, the door slams and its dial spins shut, the savings jar fills toward the visitor's goal, and the visitor either gets the thing it saved for or smiles and waves; it sinks behind the desk edge (a clip rectangle at the edge hides what has sunk; see Performance). The board's cups go out, the door swings open, and the next visitor rises with its sack and its goal while the purse tips over and the next coins slide out into the row.
- **Locks (steps 4 and 5):** instead of tags, wooden lock plates lie across the mat, each with a brass padlock and its amount drawn on the plank. Step 4 has two plates stacked; step 5 has one plate across the top of the mat with a row (or grid) of dashed slots below it, one slot per coin of the fewest-coins answer. The board shows only the locks' cups: at step 4 the first lock's amount in rows 1 to 5 (lit as the visitor fills it) and the open lock's amount, unlit, in rows 6 to 10, so the two amounts sit one above the other; at step 5 the one lock's amount from row 1. The row holds stacks that never run out (quarter, dime, nickel, penny; step 4 leaves out the quarter under 25¢). A coin dropped anywhere on the mat goes into the open lock (step 5: its next empty slot) and lights its cups; a coin the lock does not take flies there, wiggles and hops back. When the last cup lights, the lock plate pulses, the coins roll from the lock into the vault, the door swings shut and the visitor waves, as for a counted collection.
- **The trade (step 6):** when the hundredth cup lights, all hundred lit cups slide together into the middle of the board's upper half and shrink as a $1 bill grows in their place (0.6 s; 1.2 s the first time, with the helper hand pointing at the bill). The bill then lies over rows 1 to 5 and the rest of the cents pour into rows 6 to 10.
- **The symbol blocks (step 8b):** after the count-on, one tag rises with the numeral and an empty dashed box on each side of it, and two wooden blocks, one with $ and one with ¢ (in random order), rise beside it. Pressing a block sends it to its box ($ to the left box, ¢ to the right one). The right block settles there, the tag then reads in full ("$48" or "88¢") and flies to the door; the other block wiggles in its box and hops back.
- **Visitors** are content, curious or delighted, never impatient, sad or pleading.

## Saving goals (round 4)

Owner, 2026-10-05: make it fun to learn about money and to want to save it. Each visitor is saving for something and shows it: a picture of the thing (bike, kite, boat, scooter, drum or paint set, a different one from the last visitor) stands on the desk edge between the stump and the sound button, with a gift tag beside it that carries the goal amount in code-drawn numerals and the task's own $ or ¢ form ("45¢", "$1 and 25¢", "$30"). It pops in as the visitor rises.

- **The goal amount** (`makeGoal` in `rules.ts`) is a multiple of 5¢ or $5 in the step's range: 10 to 100¢ for coins up to a dollar, 105 to 195¢ above it, $5 to $100 for bills. It is never the collection's total and never one of the tags, so the goal never shows the answer. About half the time it is below the total, at least 0.6 of it (the count reaches it), otherwise above it, at most 1.6 times it (not yet). A tiny total with no round amount free takes the nearest free amount below it. Measured over 1,200 tasks per step: reached 0.47 (steps 1, 2 and 6), 0.57 (step 3), 0.48 (step 7), 0.49 (step 8).
- **Locks (steps 4 and 5):** the goal is the lock's own amount, so the jar always fills exactly to the top. A different goal beside a lock plate showing another amount would compete with the amount to build. Decision open to the owner.
- **Step 8** sets $ against ¢, so its gift tag stays blank (no symbol to give the answer away) until the task is counted; then the amount writes in with a small pop. The bills in the row already carry "$", as before.
- **Nothing is the child's and nothing persists.** The child counts for the visitor; the goal, the jar and the thing belong to the visitor and are gone with it. Goals, jars and what the visitors got are play material only: never saved, never a balance, never touching stars or stickers. The next visitor brings a new goal and an empty jar.

## The task's end: vault, jar and goal

After the matching tag flies to the door (a filled lock's plate pulses instead), on its own clock:

1. **Into the vault** (from 0.35 s): the coins and bills roll out of the dishes, pile or locks in an arc into the doorway, 40 ms apart, spinning and shrinking.
2. **Door slam** (when the last has rolled in, about 0.3 to 0.75 s later): the round door swings shut accelerating (0.3 s, ease in), lands with a `door-clunk`, overshoots a little wider and flatter and settles (0.28 s), dust puffs and a few pencil shavings hop from the doorway, and the visitor hops. Then the brass dial on the door's round plate spins two and a quarter turns and catches (0.55 s, `lock-spin`).
3. **The jar** rises beside the board on the mat's right end (0.3 s) as the door shuts. It carries the visitor's goal: the gift tag with the goal amount hangs on its neck and a small copy of the picture sits on its lid, so the jar's top is the goal. From the slam plus 0.15 s the jar fills for 0.75 s (ease out) to exactly the counted amount's share of the goal: fill height = min(1, total / goal) of the jar's inside, so it is full at the goal and never past it. The board's lit cups dim as gold motes fly from them into the jar's mouth, and four rising `jar-fill` glugs sound.
4. **The outcome** (when the fill ends):
   - **Reached** (the jar is full): glints pop over the jar; the picture springs out of the jar's lid and grows over the middle of the mat (0.35 s, springing past its size), a ribbon lands on it with a burst of leaves, shavings and glints, it rests with a soft glow until 0.8 s, then flies into the visitor's arms (0.35 s, `go`); the visitor hops and holds it as it sinks. 1.2 s.
   - **Not yet:** the visitor smiles, hops and waves (its happy pose, a gentle "next time"), the picture beside it gives a small wiggle, a few glints rise in the jar, a soft `go`. 0.9 s. The jar stays as filled; nothing says more is needed.
5. **The visitor sinks** (0.35 s) and the jar slides down and fades (0.3 s); the next visitor rises.

The whole sequence takes about 3.4 to 3.9 s. **Any press or key during it plays the rest four times faster** (about 1 s), so a child who wants to go on is never held up; the presses count for nothing. The introduction's goal-first demonstration is not sped up.

## Discovery without words

The very first round shows the goal first: the dishes already hold a dime and two pennies, the board's twelve cups are lit, a "12¢" tag sits on the door and the squirrel's goal (the bike, "10¢") stands beside the stump; the coins roll into the vault, the door slams and its dial spins, the jar fills to the top, and the bike springs out of the jar with its ribbon into the squirrel's arms. The door swings open again, the squirrel rises with its sack, the purse tips and a dime and two pennies slide into the row. While they are still sliding, the coloured-pencil helper hand rises from the bottom edge, presses the dime, carries it to the dime dish and lets go; ten cups pour. The hand then rests on the next penny and taps it in a loop until the child does anything. After the last penny the count-on plays and three tags rise; the hand does not point at a tag. The introduction's other two visitors come from the child's current step. The introduction records no evidence, changes no motor tier and always earns three stars.

Idle: after 6 quiet seconds the hand carries a see-through hint coin (in a pulsing warm halo; it never pours anything) from the row to where it goes, then fades; it repeats every 7 s while the child stays idle. While counting it shows the largest coin or bill left; at a lock, the largest coin the lock takes; at the tag pick, and at step 8's symbol blocks, it does not point: the dishes count on again from the largest instead. A task whose lock was hinted records no learning evidence; a count-on replay does not point at the answer, so a tag picked after one still counts.

**First-time demonstrations.** The first task of each step from 2 to 8 that a profile meets (not its warm-up; at step 8 its first symbol task) gets one short ghost-hand demonstration of its new idea; the save remembers which ones the profile has seen (`demos`, one bit per step), so each plays once per profile. The demonstrated task records no evidence.

- Steps 2 and 3: the hand carries the largest coin from the row into its dish (at step 3 a quarter, which pours 25 cups), then taps the next largest coin until the child acts.
- Step 4: the visitor fills the first lock itself (its coins fly from the purse into the plank); the hand then carries a different coin from the row into the second lock, then taps the next useful coin until the child acts.
- Step 5: the hand carries the largest coin the lock takes (the first coin of the fewest-coins answer) into the first slot, then taps the next one until the child acts.
- Step 6: the child counts; the first trade plays slowly (1.2 s): the hundred lit cups slide together and become a $1 bill on the board's upper half while the hand points at it.
- Step 7: the hand carries the largest bill onto the pile (twenty cups pour for a $20), then taps the next bill until the child acts.
- Step 8: the child counts; when the boxed tag and the two blocks rise, the hand presses the matching block and it flies into its box with the hand; the tag reads in full and the task ends.

## Round flow and attempts

1. **Tasks:** 4 visitors per round, or 3 at motor tier 0 on step 1. Six visitors (squirrel, rabbit, badger, hedgehog, owl, beaver) take turns, one per task. A visitor brings one collection to count (steps 1 to 3, 6 and 7), one lock task (steps 4 and 5), or one $ and ¢ task (step 8). Every round above step 1 opens with one warm-up task from the step below, which records nothing; the rest come from the current step, so each round has at least three counted tasks and a clean round can move the child up a step at every tier.
2. **Celebration:** the vault door is shut and its dial spins; leaves and pencil shavings burst from the vault and drift down across the desk; every visitor of the round (up to four) pops up along the desk edge in its happy pose, dancing (later visitors a little more), spread over the free edge left and right of the vault and clear of the corner buttons; a visitor that got its thing this round holds it. The things the round's visitors got pop onto the mat one after another, each with its ribbon, and a second, lighter fall of leaves and glints comes as the stars land one by one. Visitors still saving just wave; nothing marks them out. Input is ignored for the first 1.5 s and until the last star lands; it ends by itself at 4.6 s.
3. **Sticker choice:** while this game's six stickers are not all owned and `rewardsEnabled` is true, the shared `createStickerOffers` helper (`src/ui/sticker-offer.ts`, reused, not copied) shows two stickers with the small sticker book beside them, standing on the felt mat; its `warm` and `warmBook` run during the celebration. Same guard as every game: input ignored for 1.2 s, nothing focused, the first key only shows focus.
4. **Still rest:** the closed vault, the sticker book with the new sticker (or the round's visitors waving when there is no gift), the stars, and Again and Home of equal size and colour. `services.roundBoundary()` runs when rest is reached.

Stars: every finished round earns 3; a hopped-back coin or a wrong tag never costs a star. Awards persist once, as in Bubble Bay and Ride Fare: round count, stars and the offered pair go into the game bag's `pending` field with a unique round `id` and `save.flush()` before the celebration shows; leaving or reloading returns to the choice or the rest; leaving rest by any route clears `pending`. Coins, bills, dishes, locks and the vault are play material only and are never saved. Pause and resume through the break nudge keep play exactly where it was.

**Attempts (motor):** one carry counts once, whatever the input style. Releasing a carried coin or bill inside the mat's zone (with the tier's snap distance around the mat) is one hit; releasing it anywhere else is one miss and it slides back to its place in the row (over the row it goes back quietly; elsewhere with a soft whoosh). For a drag that includes letting go over the corner buttons. With click to carry, the second click on a corner button is the button's, not a release: on Home it goes to the hub (the piece is not counted), on the sound button it switches the sound on or off and the piece stays carried; neither counts as a hit or a miss. A press on a tag or a symbol block is one hit. A press on empty play space is one miss. A coin that hops back from a lock is still a hit. The press that picks a piece up (by a drag or a click), key presses, demonstrations and hints never count; with click to carry, the second click is the release. A press during a task's round-end sequence only speeds it up and never counts. At the end of a round: 8 or more attempts with under 70 percent hits moves down a tier; 12 or more at 90 percent or better is a qualifying round, and two in a row move up a tier.

- **Coins keep the true ratios** (dime 17.91 mm, penny 19.05, nickel 21.21, quarter 24.26): at tier 2 and 1366x768 the dime is 96 px, the penny 102, the nickel 114 and the quarter 130; at tier 0 the dime is 120 and the quarter 163. The dime never draws below 96 CSS px at any window size or tier; when `u` is smaller, coins keep that floor and the row holds fewer places.
- **Bills** keep a 2:1 shape, never below 192 x 96 CSS px in the row or in the hand and never above 280 x 140 CSS px at 1366x768 (the largest, tier 0 lifted 1.08x, is 238 x 119). Bills that only show what has been counted (on the pile, on the board, rolling into the vault) are display, like the coins in the dishes: on the pile they are drawn at play size where the mat allows (210 x 105 at 1366x768 tier 1) and smaller in small windows so every bill's numeral panel stays in view; the board's $1 is 280 x 140 at 1366x768 and 123 x 62 at 390x600.
- **Row places:** the learning step decides which coins and bills come and how many; the tier decides how many places they sit in. When there are more coins than places, coins of one kind share a place as a stack (the top coin is the target; up to four below show as offset rims). Bills never stack where the window has room (round 4): every bill gets its own place, in a second line of places when one line is too short (each bill line only as tall as a lifted bill plus 20u), so every bill's numerals stay in view; at 1366x768 and 1920x1080 a collection's 8 bills at most take two lines of 4 or 5. Only a window too narrow for that (not verified; see below) still stacks bills, and then the bills below peek out up and to the right. Places are the quarter's diameter plus 16u wide (at least 96 CSS px) by the row's height (the quarter's diameter plus 36u); in a task with bills every place is the bill's width plus 16u (in a portrait window two places share a line, 184 px each at 390x600, and the 192 px bills overlap their neighbour by 8 px). The number of pieces per collection is the tier's cap; the step's amount range is met with fewer, larger pieces when the cap is low.
- **The pile (step 2 and every spilled collection):** the coins lie closer together than their places, so neighbours overlap: centres are 0.74 of the largest coin apart (at least 96 px), every other coin sits higher or lower by 0.12 of the row's height, each is turned up to 0.45 rad and nudged up to 7u, and they are drawn in random order, so a different coin lies on top each time. Each coin still owns its own column of the row, at least 96 px wide and the row's full height, as its hit area; the highlighted coin is drawn on top.
- **Drop zone:** the whole drawn mat (plus the tier's snap distance, never over the row or the board) is the one drop target in every step, less the corner buttons' own circles where a large `uiScale` brings them over the mat's top: a coin goes to its own dish or the open lock wherever it lands on the mat, a bill to the pile or the board. The dishes, plates and slots only show where things go. The zone is 950 x 369 or more at 1366x768, 556 x 272 at 800x600 and at least 208 x 161 at 390x600 (two lines of places). Dishes grow with the coins (1.43 to 1.6 times their coin's diameter), so tier 0 dishes are larger.

## Learning steps (hidden, separate from motor)

From research doc 08, section 6, all eight steps kept as written there.

**Evidence.** Each counted task of the current step (not the warm-up) gives one result, right or not:

- A pointer choice counts when the press that made it came at least 0.7 s after the child's previous press in play. A key choice counts when the child moved the highlight with an arrow key and then waited at least 0.4 s before the action key. A choice made during a demonstration, right after a hint that pointed at the answer (a lock hint), or by mashing records nothing for that task.
- Tag tasks (steps 1 to 3, 6, 7 and 8a): right means the first deliberate tag pick was the matching tag. A task with no deliberate pick records nothing.
- Lock tasks (steps 4 and 5): right means the lock was filled with deliberate choices and no coin hopped back.
- Step 8b: right means the first deliberate block choice was the right symbol.

**Moving between steps** (between rounds only): move up one step when every counted result of the round at the current step was right and there were at least 3, or when 6 of the last 8 at the step were right; move down one step (never below step 1) when 2 or fewer of the last 6 were right. A step change resets that step's window. **No stall:** a child who stays on a step keeps getting full rounds there; a child who plays only with keys and never uses the arrows records no evidence, so after 3 finished rounds at step 1 or 2 with no counted result the step moves up by one, up to step 3, as in Ride Fare. Beyond that only evidence moves it. Struggling inside a round only brings the idle hint sooner (after 4 s instead of 6 s once two choices on the same task went wrong); the content never changes mid-round.

- **Same mix at step 4 (decision, round 2).** A same-mix completion cannot happen: the coin that would cause it hops back with a smile (rule above), so the task always ends with a different mix. The hop-back marks the task as not right for evidence; the task still finishes and earns its place in the round's three stars.
- **All eight steps are built (round 3).** The progression runs from step 1 to step 8 with no cap below the top; saves keep any step up to 8, and a save from an earlier build loads unchanged (no new fields).
- **Collections.** Coin collections start from the fewest coins for the total, then break coins into smaller ones (a dime into two nickels, a nickel into five pennies, and at steps 6 and 8 a quarter into two dimes and a nickel) toward a random count up to the tier's cap; the last quarter, dime and nickel stay whole, so a collection keeps its larger kinds. Bill collections do the same with bills ($20 into two $10s, $10 into two $5s, $5 into five $1s) up to the tier's bill cap. Totals whose fewest pieces exceed the cap are not drawn (at tier 0's six coins, step 1 totals stay under about 60¢; at its four bills, step 7 totals stay at or under $80).
- **Tags.** One tag is right; the other two differ from it by one piece's worth (1, 5, 10 or 25¢; $1, $5, $10 or $20 for bills; the collection's own pieces first), never fall below the smallest piece in the collection and never repeat each other. They stay at or under 100¢ at steps 1 to 3 and at or under $100 at step 7; at step 6 they stay above 100¢, so all three read "$1 and N¢" and none names the same amount in another form (no "125¢" next to "$1 and 25¢"). At step 8a one of them is the look-alike. Their order is shuffled. All tags of one task draw their numerals at one size (the largest at which the longest fits), so a tag's length never changes how big its numerals look.
- **Easing.** A child who struggles at any step from 2 to 8 moves back one step and gets full rounds of that content, each opening with a warm-up from the step below. Steps 1 to 3 together are a full game for a strong 6-year-old: counting mixed coins that Ride Fare already taught, sorting, and quarters.
- **Never stuck.** Every task can be finished with what is on the desk: a wrong tag fades out and the choice stays open until the matching tag is picked; locks draw from stacks that never run out, the hop-back rules above only refuse coins that could not lead to a full lock, and pennies always can; a wrong symbol block hops back and the right one stays.
- **The decimal point** is not used anywhere. Amounts over a dollar read "$1 and 25¢", whole dollars "$45", cents "45¢". This follows Common Core, Florida, New York and North Carolina (08 section 4). Texas and Virginia teach $0.45 in grade 2; it could be a step 9 later. **This is a decision open to the owner.**

## Mistake handling

There is no wrong state, and a visitor whose jar is not full yet is never a mistake: the child's count is the same task either way. A tag that does not match tilts gently twice and sinks back, fading to nothing over 0.4 s, while the visitor smiles and the dishes count on again from the largest; the other two tags stay. A coin that a lock does not take, or a symbol block that does not fit, flies to the lock or box, wiggles there and hops back to its place with a soft giggle (three quiet `pop` A notes); no error sound, no red, no shake. A coin put down off the mat (dropped, or clicked down while carried) hops gently back to its place with a `whoosh`. Coins and bills are never lost: anything not in a dish, lock or the pile always returns to the row. Presses while a coin is still pouring send nothing more; the pressed coin gives a small hop.

## Keyboard-only and mouse-only play

- **Mouse or trackpad only:** every action is a press, a drag or a click-click; any mouse button counts. The context menu is cancelled.
- **Cursor and hover (round Z1):** the scene's `hoverAt` tells the app's big cursor (`src/engine/cursor.ts`) what a press would do, using the same hit tests and gates as a press. Open hand over a coin or bill a press would pick up now (not while a piece is pouring or flying, the trade plays or the helper demonstrates). Closed hand anywhere while a piece is carried. Pointing hand over a tag that has finished rising, a symbol block that can still be sent, the corner Home, the sticker offers and Again and Home on the rest screen, anywhere during a task's round-end sequence until a press has hurried it (not in the goal-first introduction), and anywhere once the celebration can be skipped. Arrow elsewhere, including the mat, the purse and a piece that has to wait, where a press only makes something hop. The sound button reports its own hover. Hover cues, eased in and out over about 0.1 s: a soft cream ring and a 6 percent swell on the piece under the pointer (a rounded frame for a bill), a cream frame and a 5 or 6 percent swell on a tag or block, a cream frame inside the mat while a carried piece is over its drop zone, and an 8 percent swell with a cream ring on the corner Home; the sticker offers and Again and Home show the keyboard focus ring under the pointer, as before. Every swell is a scale transform of the cached art; the rings are one path stroke each.
- **Keyboard only:** a bobbing arrow and a warm ring mark the highlighted piece.
  - Counting: the highlight starts on the largest coin or bill in the row (leftmost of that kind). Left and Right (Up and Down step the same way) move through every piece in the row. Any other key sends the highlighted piece to its dish or the pile (at most one every 150 ms; none while a piece is still on its way); the highlight then moves to the largest piece left.
  - Tags: when the tags rise the highlight is on the left tag. Arrows move between the three tags; any other key picks the highlighted one. A wrong tag fades and the highlight moves to the next tag still there.
  - Locks: the highlight starts on the largest coin the open lock takes. Arrows move through the four stacks; any other key sends the highlighted coin into the open lock.   - Step 8b: the highlight starts on the left block; arrows switch blocks; any other key moves the highlighted block into the box.
  - During play every key plays, Escape, Tab and Enter included. After the round, Escape goes home and Tab reaches the corner Home and sound buttons; choice and rest follow the shared rule (first key shows focus, arrows move, the next key chooses).
- A piece lifted with a key stays a key carry if the mouse then drops it; it is never a motor attempt.

## Currency illustrations

Research doc 08 section 5 is the finding; summary here. 18 U.S.C. 504 permits illustrations of US currency only under conditions: black and white, or (under 31 CFR 411.1) colour if one-sided, under 75 percent or over 150 percent of the real note's size in each dimension, and with every file used to make it destroyed after final use. 18 U.S.C. 474 makes it a crime to make an impression "in the likeness of" a note or any part of it outside those exceptions. A published web game keeps its image files for as long as it is live, so it could never meet the destruction condition, and 504(2) withholds the exception from electronic reproduction anyway. The game therefore stays outside "illustration" and "likeness" altogether: its bills are original toy art, not pictures of real notes. In our reading (not legal advice) such bills carry no meaningful risk, so the owner's stop condition was not triggered.

Limits every bill in this game keeps (08 section 5, "Design limits"):

1. Original art only; the image model never saw a real note (the only reference attached was the game's own concept, which shows a toy bill).
2. No portrait of any person (a friendly animal silhouette instead), no seals, serial numbers, signatures, lettering or numerals in the art, no lathe-work or guilloche borders, no corner circles or inner frame echoing a real note's layout, no buildings, no security thread.
3. Not a real note's colours: one butter-cream toy palette for all four, never green and black, purple, orange or green and peach.
4. Squat 2:1 shape with rounded corners (a real note is about 2.35:1). The generated bills came out 2.21 to 2.42:1 and were cut to exactly 2:1 by removing a strip from each plain side panel (no stretching).
5. Drawn at most 280 x 140 CSS px at 1366x768 (largest drawn here: 238 x 119 lifted at tier 0; the $1 on the board 256 x 128, 271 x 136 in its count-on pulse).
6. One side only; bills never flip.
7. A thick soft outline, flat colour and a big code-drawn numeral make it plainly a toy.
8. No bill-shaped advertising or promotional art (18 U.S.C. 475).

Coins look like real US coins, the same set Piggy Parade uses (round RC1, 2026-10-06). The owner asked for it: "the coins need to be significantly more USA-actual change looking. currently you can not tell what a dime vs nickel is for example". Pictures of coins are allowed: 18 U.S.C. 489 covers physical tokens in the likeness of a coin, and a 1951 amendment removed the language that could restrict coin pictures in publications (08 section 5). Each coin shows its real motif in soft metal relief: a copper penny with Lincoln and the shield, a smooth-edged silver nickel with Jefferson and Monticello, a smaller reeded silver dime with Roosevelt and the torch between branches, and a reeded silver quarter with Washington and the eagle. No inscriptions, lettering, numbers or dates on any face.

## Performance

No allocation in update or render: coins and bills in flight (32), pieces rolling into the vault (64) and the effect bits (160: glints, leaves, shavings, dust and the jar's motes, typed arrays in `fx.ts`, drawn from eight sprites baked once per layout unit) are pooled; effects take their scatter from `Math.random`, so they never shift the seeded task sequence; amounts are laid out in one preallocated glyph sequence. Every sprite is drawn at a fixed size per layout and animated with transforms (the dish, lock-plate and bill pulses, the lifted tag and blocks, and the dish coins scale one cached size; no pulse makes a new size); one cached scaled canvas per sprite size, released when the canvas size, pixel ratio, tier or fit changes, and on exit. The mat's three slices, the unlit and lit cups, step 5's dashed slot ring, the rings under dish coins, the four bills with their numerals and the board's $1, the hint halo, the carried coin's and bill's soft shadows, the vault dial, the jar's hatched fill, the bit sprites, and the glyph strips with their symbol box are baked on CPU canvases (`willReadFrequently: true`, see pitfalls) when their size changes, never per frame; each bake step ends with `getImageData(0, 0, 1, 1)`. Tag, lock, door and block numerals are copied glyph by glyph from the strips (image copies, no text drawing); bill numerals are part of each bill's baked canvas. The background is rescaled only when the canvas size or pixel ratio changes, and its cover-fit is computed from the loaded image when the scaled copy is made (pitfall 2026-10-04). No gradients, `shadowBlur` or `fillText` per frame. The door swing, its slam bounce and the dial's spin are transforms of the cached door and dial. The jar's fill shows the bottom share of its one baked canvas (a source rectangle), the goal picture in the band, on the jar's lid, over the mat and in the visitor's arms is drawn from two cached sizes (band and big) with a scale transform, and the getting-the-thing pictures, the jar, the ribbon and the gift tag are scaled ahead in idle periods with the rest. Measured round 4 at 1366x768 (production build, offscreen headed Chrome): scene workMean 0.35 to 0.50 ms and workMax at most 4 ms during the task's end with bits and the jar, 0.7 ms during the celebration; no frame over 50 ms. The visitor is drawn whole inside one clip rectangle whose bottom is the desk edge plus the sack's hang times how far the visitor has risen: standing, nothing is cut (the sack hangs over the desk); rising and sinking, the part below the edge is hidden, so the visitor goes behind the desk. A clip per frame allocates nothing. Celebration, choice and rest art is scaled ahead in idle periods as in Ride Fare (4 ms rule, 500 ms cap). Target: scene `workMean` under 0.5 ms and `workMax` under 3 ms at 1366x768 after the first 3 s. A task's first frame lays the task out again: measured round 5 over the first second of step 7 tasks at 1366x768, `workMax` 3 to 7 ms (it was 11 to 32 ms when the first bill task baked the bills' strip and canvases, and every change of the row's lines rebaked the lock and board strips).

The eight coin faces are byte-for-byte copies of `public/art/piggy-parade/coin-*-{heads,tails}.webp` (320x320, made for Piggy Parade on 2026-10-04 with the same Codex `image_gen` tool; prompts, raw images and processing are recorded in `docs/games/piggy-parade.md` under "Art": square box around the disc's centre, exact round alpha edge, WebP quality 80, alpha quality 50). The largest coin this game draws at 1920x1080 is 258 px (the tier-0 quarter, 228 px, lifted), under the files' 320 px, so no new larger set was needed.

Processing (`sharp`; scripts kept with the sources): regions of alpha above 128 found and isolated, alpha under 16 cleared and over 235 made solid, colours at soft edges replaced by nearby solid colours (no dark or light fringe), specks under 300 px removed, cropped to the alpha bounds with 4 px padding, resized with Lanczos 3 and written as WebP quality 80, alpha quality 50. The two poses of each visitor share one scale (waiting pose 400 px tall). Bills: cropped, then cut from 2.21 to 2.42:1 to exactly 2:1 by removing one vertical strip from each plain side panel (at 0.2 and 0.8 of the width, 48 px crossfade at each join), then resized to 640x320. Dishes, the tag and the symbol block were cut from shared sheets. Every file was checked on light, mid-grey and dark grounds for halos and stray pixels (contact sheet with the sources).

`desk.webp` follows the three-strip method (PR #13, Ride Fare): the 1536x1024 base `bg-base3` (third attempt: the first was too saturated and smooth for coloured pencil, the second put the desk edge too high) was cut into three overlapping 683x1024 strips, each enlarged to 1024x1536 and redrawn faithfully in its own request (`bg-s0-1`, `bg-s1-1`, `bg-s2-1`, concept and strip attached). The PR #13 stitch script registered each strip on the base enlarged 1.5 times (alignment error 9.6 to 12.5), locked broad colour to the base (Gaussian sigma 32), joined neighbours along the least-different vertical path (6 px feather) and resized the 2304x1536 mosaic to 1920x1280 (Lanczos 3), WebP quality 80, opaque. A straight-line scan finds no vertical seam (only the plank joins). It shows only the forest floor behind the desk (moss, ferns, ivy and pinecones toward the corners) and the empty desk: no stump, mat, dishes, purse, coins, bills or animals.
